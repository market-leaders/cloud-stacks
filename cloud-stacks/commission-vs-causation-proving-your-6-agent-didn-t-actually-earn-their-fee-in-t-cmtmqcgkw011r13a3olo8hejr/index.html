The 6% Commission Is for Presence, Not Performance

The traditional 6% real estate commission is often paid for simply being present, not for directly causing the sale. This model was built for a world that no longer exists, yet sellers continue to pay the price for its inefficiency. It's a system that rewards participation over actual value creation.

What is "Procuring Cause"? The Outdated Standard for Commissions

Procuring Cause: An outdated real estate concept stating that the agent who initiated an unbroken chain of events that led to a sale is the one who "earned" the commission. In the pre-internet era, this made sense. An agent might introduce a buyer from their personal Rolodex or show them an exclusive office listing.

Today, this concept is ambiguous and irrelevant. According to the National Association of Realtors, 90% of recent buyers used the internet in their home search. When a buyer's Zillow alert does the work, who is the real procuring cause? The argument that an agent's presence alone justifies a massive percentage of your home's value is fundamentally broken.

The Justification vs. The Reality

Traditional agents justify their 6% fee with claims of high "marketing costs," brokerage splits, and expertise. Online Platforms Are the Real "Cause" of Your Sale

In today's market, powerful online platforms—not the listing agent—are the primary 'cause' for a buyer discovering your home. The agent's role has transformed, but their commission structure has stubbornly refused to adapt, leaving sellers to foot the bill for work technology now does for free.

The Zillow & MLS Syndication Machine

Here’s how modern real estate "marketing" actually works: an agent enters your home's data into the local Multiple Listing Service (MLS) one single time. The MLS then automatically syndicates that listing to hundreds of websites, including Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin, and every competing brokerage site in your area. This massive exposure is the result of a system, not a sustained, expensive marketing campaign by one agent. The agent's primary task here is accurate data entry.

From Information Gatekeeper to Transaction Coordinator

The role of a listing agent has fundamentally changed. They are no longer gatekeepers of information who hold exclusive access to listings. They are transaction coordinators who manage scheduling, advise on offers, navigate paperwork, and coordinate with lenders, appraisers, and inspectors. This work requires skill and is absolutely essential for a smooth closing. However, it does not justify a percentage-based fee that was designed for the old role of "finding the buyer." The things a great real estate agent should do have evolved, and the fee structure should too.

The Buyer's Agent Commission: A Necessary Incentive

It's important to acknowledge that a significant portion of the total commission (typically 2.5-3%) goes to the agent who brings the buyer. This is a crucial incentive to encourage buyer's agents to show your property. The 1 Percent Lists model intelligently focuses on reducing the *listing side* of the commission while still offering a competitive commission to the buyer's agent. This ensures maximum exposure and cooperation from all agents in the market, giving you the best of both worlds.
