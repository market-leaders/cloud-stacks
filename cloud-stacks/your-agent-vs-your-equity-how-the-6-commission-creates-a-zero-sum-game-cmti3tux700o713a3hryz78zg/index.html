The Problem: The 6% Commission is a Zero-Sum Game for Your Equity

The traditional 6% real estate commission directly reduces a homeowner's net proceeds, creating an inherent conflict between the seller's financial goals and the agent's income.

The Zero-Sum Game Explained: Your Equity vs. Their Commission

In a zero-sum game, one person's gain is equivalent to another's loss. When applied to real estate commissions, the math is brutally simple. On a $500,000 home sale, a 6% commission is $30,000. If you negotiate that down to 5%, the commission is $25,000. That $5,000 difference doesn't come from thin air—it comes directly out of your net proceeds. The agent wins $5,000, you lose $5,000. While technology and market efficiency have changed the game, the commission structure hasn't caught up.

Where Does the 6% Really Go? A Breakdown for Homeowners

That 6% fee isn't a single paycheck for your agent. Typically, it's split down the middle: 3% to the listing agent's brokerage and 3% to the buyer's agent's brokerage. From there, the agent you hired splits their 3% with their broker, often a 50/50 split. This means your agent might only see 1.5% of the total sale price. This inefficient model forces agents to charge high fees to cover massive franchise overhead and outdated systems, all at your expense.

The median home price in the U.S. hovers around $400,000, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. Let's see how the traditional commission impacts a sale at that price point.
