Why is ponding water on my commercial roof a bigger problem than it looks?

Ponding water on a commercial flat roof is a serious threat because it accelerates material degradation, adds significant weight, and inevitably leads to costly leaks. That seemingly harmless puddle is actively working to break down your roofing materials, creating a direct path for water to enter your building, damage inventory, and disrupt tenants.

The 48-Hour Rule: When a Puddle Becomes a Problem

The roofing industry has a clear standard: if water is still standing on your roof 48 hours after it stops raining, you have a ponding issue. This isn't just an aesthetic concern; it's a clear indicator that your roof's drainage system is failing. This is one of the most common commercial roof issues we see from New Orleans to Gulfport.

From Puddle to Structural Damage: The Escalating Risks

The danger of ponding water grows over time. First, it magnifies UV rays, breaking down the roof membrane. Next, it finds a way through seams or cracks, leading to the need to find a roof leak. This water then saturates the insulation, rendering it useless and adding immense weight. Finally, it begins to rot the underlying roof deck, compromising the structural integrity of your entire building.

The Business Cost: Interruption, Inventory Loss, and Tenant Complaints

For a property manager, a roof leak is never just a leak. It's a potential lawsuit from a slip-and-fall, a damaged shipment in a warehouse, a ruined server room, or an angry tenant demanding rent concessions. Proactively addressing ponding water is a direct investment in protecting your bottom line.

What causes flat roof ponding in Louisiana and Mississippi specifically?

The primary causes of flat roof ponding in the Gulf South are clogged drains from storm debris and structural settling, both worsened by our heavy rainfall. Our region's frequent storms and high humidity create a perfect storm for drainage failures and accelerated material decay on commercial properties.

Clogged Drains, Scuppers, and Gutters

This is the most common and easily preventable cause. Debris from oak trees in Mandeville, wind-blown trash in Baton Rouge, and sediment from deteriorating roof surfaces can quickly block your roof's primary drainage points, forcing water to pool.

Structural Deflection and Low Spots

Over time, buildings settle. This can cause the roof deck to sag or "deflect" in certain areas, creating natural low spots where water collects. This is especially common on large-span commercial buildings like warehouses and retail centers found along the Mississippi Gulf Coast.

HVAC and Equipment Complications

Improperly installed HVAC units, satellite dishes, or other rooftop equipment can create dams that obstruct water flow. Additionally, clogged condensation lines from air conditioning units can create persistent puddles even on dry days, a year-round problem in our humid climate.

The Gulf South Factor: How Our Climate Accelerates Damage

The combination of intense, sudden downpours and year-round humidity puts immense stress on flat roofing systems. This climate not only causes more frequent ponding but also creates the perfect environment for algae and vegetation to grow in standing water, which can further degrade the roof membrane.
