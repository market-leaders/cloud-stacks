Telltale Signs You Need to Call a Roofer

Don't wait for a drip to become a downpour. If you notice any of the following red flags, it's time to contact a professional for an inspection:

  • Visible water damage: Stains, streaks, or moisture on ceilings or in the attic are clear indicators of a leak.
  • Damaged shingles: Look for shingles that are missing, cracked, curling at the edges, or buckling.
  • Granules in gutters: Asphalt shingles shed granules as they age. An excessive amount in your gutters means they are nearing the end of their lifespan.
  • Sagging roof deck: A drooping or sagging roofline can signal a serious structural problem that requires immediate attention.
  • Advanced age: Most asphalt shingle roofs last around 20-25 years. If your roof is approaching this age, it’s wise to have it professionally evaluated.

Repair vs. Replacement: Making the Right Call for Your Home

Once a problem is identified, the next question is whether you need a simple repair or a full replacement. The answer depends on the age of your roof and the extent of the damage. Understanding this distinction is key to making a cost-effective decision.

A repair is often sufficient for isolated damage on a relatively new roof, such as replacing a few missing shingles after a storm. However, if the damage is widespread, your roof is over two decades old, or it already has multiple layers of shingles, a full replacement is typically the smarter long-term investment. Investing in modern materials, like a fortified roof, can also provide superior protection and even lower insurance costs.

5 Must-Ask Questions for Any Roofing Contractor

Before you sign any contract, arm yourself with the right questions. A reputable roofer will be happy to provide clear and confident answers. If they hesitate or evade, consider it a red flag.

  1. Are you licensed and insured in this state? Ask for proof of both general liability insurance and workers' compensation.
  2. Do you provide a detailed, written estimate? The estimate should break down all costs for labor, materials, permits, and cleanup.
  3. What warranties do you offer? You should receive two: a manufacturer's warranty on materials and a workmanship warranty from the contractor.
  4. How will you protect my property during the project? A professional crew will use tarps and take precautions to protect your landscaping, siding, and driveway.
  5. Can you provide references from recent jobs in my area? Speaking to past customers is one of the best ways to gauge a contractor's reliability and quality.

Decoding a Roofing Estimate: What to Look For

A professional estimate is more than just a final price. It’s a detailed document that outlines the entire scope of the project. A transparent and thorough estimate is a hallmark of a trustworthy contractor.

  • Itemized Materials: The type of shingles, underlayment, flashing, and ventilation should be specified.
  • Labor Costs: A clear breakdown of the cost for the crew's time.
  • Permit Fees: The cost of pulling any necessary local building permits.
  • Debris Removal: Fees associated with the disposal of the old roof and construction waste.
  • Payment Schedule: A clear outline of when payments are due, typically tied to project milestones.
