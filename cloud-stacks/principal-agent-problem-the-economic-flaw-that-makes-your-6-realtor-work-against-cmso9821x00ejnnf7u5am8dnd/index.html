What is the Principal-Agent Problem in Simple Terms?

In economics, the "principal" (you, the homeowner) hires an "agent" (your Realtor) to perform a task. The problem arises when the agent has an incentive to act in their own best interest, even if it's not in the principal's best interest. In real estate, your goal is to maximize your sale price. Your agent's goal, under the 6% model, is often the fastest commission check with the least amount of effort.

How the 6% Commission Breaks Down (and Breaks Trust)

Let's do the math. Assume a 6% commission, which is typically split between the buyer's and seller's agents (3% each). Your agent then splits their 3% share with their brokerage. The numbers reveal a shocking lack of motivation for them to fight for your last dollar. Consider this common scenario for a home listed at $500,000: * **You receive an early offer for $490,000.** It's a decent offer, but you suspect you could get more. * **Your Agent's Choice:** They can advise you to accept the $490k offer for a quick and easy close. Or, they can spend several more weeks holding open houses, marketing, and negotiating to secure a $510,000 offer. That extra $20,000 is a massive win for you.
