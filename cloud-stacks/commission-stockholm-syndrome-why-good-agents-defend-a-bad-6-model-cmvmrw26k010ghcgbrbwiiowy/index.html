The Diagnosis: What is Commission Stockholm Syndrome?

Many talented real estate agents are trapped in a high-commission system due to brokerage fee structures, industry-wide pressure, and the misconception that high fees equal better service.

This isn't a simple case of greed. It's a systemic problem that has created a generation of agents who, like hostages, have begun to identify with their captors—the inefficient, high-cost brokerage models that dictate their income. They defend the very system that limits their potential and hurts their clients' bottom line.

The Brokerage Bind: How Commission Splits Keep Agents Captive

The math behind a real estate commission is the first clue. That 6% fee doesn't go into your agent's pocket. It's typically split four ways: the listing agent, their broker, the buyer's agent, and the buyer's agent's broker. Suddenly, the 3% your listing agent is "charging" becomes 1.5% or even less after their broker takes a hefty cut, which can be as high as 50%.

This structure forces agents to cling to a high commission rate just to make a living wage. They aren't defending 6% because they believe it reflects their value in 2024; they're defending it because their brokerage model leaves them no other choice. It's a volume game where the house—the traditional brokerage—always wins.

The Industry Echo Chamber: "This is How It's Always Been Done"

The real estate industry is notoriously resistant to change. For decades, traditional brokerages have cultivated a culture where high commissions are equated with quality and professionalism. Agents who dare to question this are often labeled as "discount" brokers, a term used to imply inferior service.

This industry echo chamber creates immense pressure to conform. New and struggling agents are taught that the 6% model is the only path to success, while veteran agents fear that breaking from the norm will tarnish their reputation. It’s a powerful narrative that stifles innovation and keeps consumers overpaying.

The Value Myth: Defending High Fees with Outdated Logic

You've heard the justifications: "You get what you pay for," or "I need that commission for my extensive marketing budget." These arguments might have held water in 1995 when marketing meant expensive print ads in local magazines and newspapers. Today, they are obsolete.

Modern digital marketing is exponentially more efficient and cost-effective. With MLS syndication, a listing is instantly pushed to Zillow, Realtor.com, and hundreds of other sites. Targeted social media ads can reach thousands of qualified buyers for a fraction of the cost of a single full-page print ad. The idea that an agent needs a $20,000 commission for "marketing" a home is a myth perpetuated to justify an outdated fee structure.
