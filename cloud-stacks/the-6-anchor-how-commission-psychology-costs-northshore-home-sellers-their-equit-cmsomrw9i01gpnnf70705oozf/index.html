The Problem: An Outdated Commission Model

Understanding "Anchoring Bias" in Real Estate

Anchoring bias is the human tendency to rely heavily on the first piece of information offered (the "anchor") when making decisions. In real estate, the first number a seller typically hears is "6%." This number becomes the baseline, making all other options seem unusual or inferior, even though commissions have always been negotiable. The 6% anchor makes sellers hesitant to push back, costing them dearly.

"But Isn't 6% Just the Cost of Full Service?"

This is a common and understandable question, but it’s rooted in a bygone era. The 6% model became standard in a pre-internet world that required immense manual effort—placing print ads, compiling physical "listing books," and relying on word-of-mouth. Today, technology has revolutionized the process. The MLS, syndication to sites like Zillow and Realtor.com, and targeted social media marketing have streamlined everything, drastically reducing an agent's overhead and time commitment. The work has changed, but the price hasn't.
