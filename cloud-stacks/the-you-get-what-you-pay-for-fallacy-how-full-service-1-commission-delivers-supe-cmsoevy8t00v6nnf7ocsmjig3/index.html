The Fallacy: Deconstructing the 6% Commission Myth in Castle Rock

Castle Rock homeowners often believe a higher commission equals better service, but this is a common and costly misconception in today's real estate market.

The belief that a 6% commission is the "standard" or necessary price for quality is one of the most persistent myths in real estate. It's a relic of a bygone era, before technology streamlined marketing and communication. Understanding where that money goes is the first step to realizing you have better options.

Where Does a Traditional 6% Commission Actually Go?

The traditional model typically splits the commission, with 3% going to the listing brokerage and 3% to the buyer's brokerage. However, a large portion of that 3% listing fee doesn't go to the agent for services rendered on your home. It often covers the high overhead of "big box" brokerages: expensive brick-and-mortar office leases, franchise fees, administrative bloat, and corporate marketing costs that don't directly benefit your specific home sale. You can learn more by comparing traditional vs. discount commissions.

The Psychology of "Paying More is Better"

Many sellers understandably fear that paying less will result in a subpar experience. They ask, "If I pay less, will my agent be less motivated? Will my home get less exposure?" This is a valid concern, but it's misplaced. An agent's primary motivation is a successful sale and a happy, reference-providing client. Modern marketing tools, like the MLS and its syndication to sites like Realtor.com, have leveled the playing field, ensuring massive exposure regardless of the commission percentage.
