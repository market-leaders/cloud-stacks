Castle Rock sellers may pay title, settlement, tax, mortgage, HOA, and contract-negotiated expenses beyond the listing fee. Which costs apply—and who pays them—depends on the transaction and the written agreement between the parties. ### Title and settlement charges Some charges may relate to title work, settlement services, recording, or other steps needed to complete the transfer. Don’t assume a particular fee is always paid by the seller: check the draft statement and purchase agreement for the exact allocation. For a broader overview of how closing costs can be divided, see [who pays closing costs](https://www.1percentmilehigh.com/buying-a-home/who-pays-closing-costs/). ### Douglas County property-tax adjustments Property taxes may be prorated between the buyer and seller based on the closing date and the applicable tax information. The amount depends on the property, tax year, and closing date, so it should be estimated using property-specific figures rather than a general rule. For example, a seller closing on a different date may have a different tax adjustment than a neighbor who sells later in the year—even if both homes are in Castle Rock. The settlement statement should show the calculation used for the transaction. ### Mortgage payoff and lien-related costs Your mortgage payoff is based on the amount owed to the lender, not simply the balance displayed on an earlier monthly statement. Interest may accrue through the payoff date, and the lender’s statement may include applicable payoff or recording charges. Other liens that affect the property may also need to be addressed before the sale can close. Request a current payoff statement from your lender as you prepare to list, then update it when the expected closing date becomes clearer. If you have questions about a lien or payoff figure, confirm the amount with the relevant lender or lienholder. ### HOA and property-specific expenses An HOA may charge for account statements, document delivery, transfer processing, or other services, and unpaid dues or assessments may affect the sale. The exact charges depend on the association and its management company. Ask the HOA or management company for its current fee schedule and any requirements for transferring ownership. Castle Rock has a range of communities and property types, so sellers should verify the rules for their own home rather than relying on a fee estimate from another neighborhood. The [Castle Rock neighborhoods guide](https://www.1percentmilehigh.com/buying-a-home/castle-rock-neighborhoods/) offers local context for sellers and buyers exploring the area. ### Negotiated buyer concessions A seller may agree to provide a buyer credit or other concession as part of an offer, but these terms are negotiable and are not automatic. Any seller contribution toward buyer-agent compensation is also optional and negotiable, subject to the purchase agreement. This is one reason to compare offers by their estimated net proceeds, not just by the proposed sale price. Two offers with similar prices can leave a seller with different estimated proceeds if their financing terms, requested credits, or other conditions differ. For more on selling expenses, see [the cost of selling a house](https://www.1percentmilehigh.com/sell-my-home-cheap/cost-of-selling-a-house/). ## How Can Castle Rock Sellers Estimate Their Net Proceeds Before Listing? These documents make a Castle Rock seller net proceeds estimate more useful than a broad online calculator, though the final settlement statement may still differ.
