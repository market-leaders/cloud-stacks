That 6% figure isn't arbitrary, but its breakdown reveals a system with built-in inefficiencies. Typically, the commission is split down the middle: 3% goes to the listing agent's brokerage (the agent representing you, the seller) and 3% goes to the buyer's agent's brokerage. But it doesn't stop there. From that 3% share, your listing agent's brokerage takes a significant cut, often 30-50%. The remaining portion is what the agent actually receives. This amount must then cover all business expenses, including the marketing for your specific property. When you see how the pie is sliced, it becomes clear that a surprisingly small fraction of that hefty commission is directly invested in selling your home. This entire framework was established before the internet, Zillow, and social media democratized information and streamlined the home search process for buyers across the world and right here in [Chattanooga's unique neighborhoods](https://sell4one.com/buy/chattanooga-neighborhoods/). ### The "Marketing" You're Paying For: Myth vs. Reality To justify their 3% fee, traditional agents often present a list of marketing activities. This playbook usually includes putting a sign in your yard, creating simple printed flyers, entering the home into the MLS, and maybe holding an occasional open house. While these steps aren't worthless, their value has diminished dramatically in the digital age. The real heavy lifting today is done by the Multiple Listing Service (MLS). Once your home is in the MLS, its data is automatically syndicated to major real estate portals like [Realtor.com](https://www.realtor.com), Zillow, and Trulia. This is where the overwhelming majority of today's buyers begin their search. This powerful, automated syndication is the single most effective marketing tool, and it certainly doesn't cost 3% of your home's value to activate. You have to ask yourself: are a yard sign and some paper flyers really worth $12,000 on a $400,000 home in Hixson or Ooltewah?
