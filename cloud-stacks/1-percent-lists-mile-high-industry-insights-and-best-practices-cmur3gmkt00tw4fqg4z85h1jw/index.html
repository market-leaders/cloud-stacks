Generally, 2.5-3% goes to the listing agent's brokerage (the agent representing you, the seller), and 2.5-3% goes to the buyer's agent's brokerage. While you only hire the listing agent, you are typically responsible for paying both sides of the commission from your sale proceeds. Let's quantify this with a realistic Castle Rock example. With the median home price in the area often hovering around $750,000, the commission breakdown is staggering. A 3% listing fee alone amounts to $22,500. When you add the buyer's agent commission, the total can easily reach $45,000. This is the specific cost that modern, efficient brokerage models like ours are designed to reduce. The traditional model was established before the internet, Zillow, and instant access to market data; today, the labor involved doesn't always justify such a high percentage-based fee. ### How High Commissions Directly Erode Your Home Equity Your home equity is the difference between your home's market value and your outstanding mortgage balance. It represents your financial stake in the property—the wealth you've built through mortgage payments and market appreciation. When you sell, the commission is deducted directly from this equity, reducing your final net profit. Think of it as the most significant and, importantly, the most negotiable closing cost. An extra $15,000 or $20,000 saved from commissions is not just a number on a settlement statement; it's a tangible asset. For a Castle Rock family, that savings could represent a larger down payment on their next home in one of the best suburbs of Denver, a significant contribution to a child's college fund, or the capital for a much-needed renovation on a new property.
