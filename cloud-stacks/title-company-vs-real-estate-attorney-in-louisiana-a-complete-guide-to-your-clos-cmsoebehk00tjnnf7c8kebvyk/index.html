Understanding the Fundamental Roles in a Louisiana Closing

In Louisiana, a real estate attorney is legally required to oversee key parts of your property closing, a mandate that fundamentally shapes your closing team and makes your choice of representation a critical decision for your financial protection. This legal requirement, rooted in our state's unique civil law tradition, means that while title companies play a vital administrative role, the attorney's legal oversight is indispensable. Understanding the specific duties of each helps clarify why an integrated approach is superior.

What a Title Company Does: The Investigator

A title company acts as a neutral third party focused exclusively on the property's title history. Think of them as the meticulous detectives of your real estate transaction. Their primary responsibilities are administrative and investigative, ensuring the chain of ownership is clear and insurable.

  • Title Search: This is the core function. The company examines public records—deeds, mortgages, court records, tax records, and more—to trace the property's ownership history. They are looking for any "clouds" or defects, such as an outstanding mortgage, an unresolved lien from a contractor, or a previously unknown heir with a claim to the property.
  • Issuing Title Insurance: Based on the findings of the search, the company issues title insurance policies. A Lender's Policy is almost always required by the mortgage company to protect their investment, while an Owner's Policy protects you, the buyer, from future claims or defects related to the title that were missed during the search.
  • Escrow Services: The title company often acts as an escrow agent, holding the buyer's earnest money and other funds securely in a trust account. They then disburse these funds to the appropriate parties at closing according to the contract and lender instructions.

However, a standalone title company's role stops there. If they uncover a serious legal problem, they can only report it; they cannot legally advise you on how to fix it or take legal action to resolve it.

What a Real Estate Attorney Does: The Protector

A real estate attorney's role is to be your legal advocate, ensuring the entire transaction is not just administratively correct but legally sound. In Louisiana, their involvement is not optional. An attorney can perform all the functions of a title company, but their capabilities extend much further into the realm of legal protection.

  • Providing a Title Opinion: This is a legally required document in Louisiana where a licensed attorney, after reviewing the title search (or "abstract"), renders a professional legal opinion certifying the status of the title. A non-attorney cannot issue this opinion.
  • Legal Counsel & Advice: This is the most significant difference. Your attorney can answer your legal questions, explain your rights and obligations under the purchase agreement, and advise you on the best course of action if a dispute arises. A title company employee is legally prohibited from giving legal advice.
  • Contract Review & Drafting: Your attorney reviews the purchase agreement and all other closing documents to ensure they protect your interests and are legally enforceable. They can also draft specific documents needed to cure title defects, such as an Act of Donation or a Power of Attorney.
  • Resolving Complex Legal Issues: This is where an attorney is invaluable. They have the authority and expertise to handle serious problems that would halt a closing in its tracks. This includes navigating a complex succession, negotiating the release of a judgment, or resolving a boundary dispute with a neighbor.
