What is RAG Poisoning and How Can It Hurt My Local Business?

RAG poisoning is a deliberate attack where competitors inject false data into AI knowledge bases, causing AI Overviews to confidently misrepresent your business hours, services, or reputation. For a local business, this means an AI could tell a potential customer that your plumbing service is closed on Saturdays, your law firm doesn't handle their specific case type, or that a competitor's inferior service is the "best in town." All of this is based on contaminated data scraped from low-authority directories, fake review sites, or subtly altered content on third-party blogs.

The Microdynamics of a Digital Slander Campaign

This isn't a brute-force hack. It's a death by a thousand paper cuts.

  • Fact: AI models are not all-knowing. They are retrieval engines. They fetch data from the web to answer user queries.
  • Fact: They rely on external, often unvetted, sources to build their worldview.
  • The Attack: A competitor can create a network of seemingly legitimate local citations, forum posts, or Q&A site entries that contain subtle inaccuracies about your business. For example, listing your service area as 10% smaller than it is, or quoting a price for your services that is artificially high. The AI, seeking consensus, ingests this "data," and your digital reputation is silently eroded.

How Do I Make My Business the Definitive Source of Truth for AI?

You must make your business the definitive source of truth by transforming your website into a machine-readable knowledge graph that AI models can parse without ambiguity, establishing data supremacy. The goal is to create such an overwhelming signal of accurate, organized information that the background noise of the internet—and your competitors' disinformation—is driven down to a noise floor beneath the depths of hell. This is the core of Generative Engine Optimization (GEO).

Step 1: Execute Flawless Entity Mapping

Your business is an "entity"—a distinct concept with attributes (name, address, phone, services, service area). Your defense begins by defining this entity with absolute precision.

  1. Consolidate Your NAP-W: Your Name, Address, Phone Number, and Website URL must be 100% consistent everywhere. Not just on your site, but across every high-authority citation.
  2. Structure Your Service Data: Use Schema.org markup to explicitly define every service you offer. Don't just write "We fix leaky pipes"; use schema:Service to label "Leaky Pipe Repair" with its serviceType, serviceArea (geo-fenced to the specific neighborhoods you serve), and provider.
  3. Build a Local Knowledge Graph: Interlink your location pages, service pages, and articles. Create a logical, crawlable hierarchy that tells a story: "This business entity operates at this address, provides these specific services to these geographic locations, and is demonstrated to be an authority through these case studies and articles."

Step 2: Engineer Your Content for Retrieval

AI doesn't "read" your blog posts; it retrieves answers. Every piece of content must be engineered to be the most citable, self-contained answer to a specific question.

  • Chunk Your Content: Break down monolithic pages into distinct, question-and-answer passages under specific headings. An AI is far more likely to cite a clean, concise paragraph answering "What Is the Cost of a Dental Implant in Austin?" than to parse a 2,000-word article on "Implant Dentistry."
  • Attribute Every Claim: When you state a fact, especially a technical one, link to its source or your supporting data. This creates a chain of trust that AI models are being trained to recognize and reward. You are building the supply chain for AI citations, and your website is the factory floor.
