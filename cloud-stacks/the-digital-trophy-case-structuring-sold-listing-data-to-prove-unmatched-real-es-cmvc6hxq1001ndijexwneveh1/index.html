The strategy isn't just to *keep* the data, but to *architect* it. This involves creating a logical, scalable technical infrastructure that makes sense to both users and search engines, turning individual sales into a powerful, interconnected network of local content. ### Step 1: Create a Permanent URL Structure for Every Sold Property The technical foundation begins with moving away from transient IDX URLs. For every property you sell, create a permanent page on your own domain, such as `yourwebsite.com/sold/123-main-street-anytown`. This URL should never change or be deleted, allowing it to accumulate SEO value indefinitely. This page should contain the property's final sold price, key details, a high-quality photo gallery, and a brief case study or "sale story." This narrative element transforms a simple data point into a compelling piece of content that showcases your marketing prowess. ### Step 2: Build Geo-Targeted "Trophy Case" Pillar Pages This is where you aggregate your proof using a hub-and-spoke model. Create pillar pages for the specific locations you dominate. These hubs serve as central points of authority for neighborhoods, zip codes, or even specific property types. Examples of pillar pages include: * `yourwebsite.com/neighborhoods/oakwood-heights` * `yourwebsite.com/zip-codes/70115` * `yourwebsite.com/condos/downtown-luxury-condos` These pages should feature a map, a brief description of the area, and a dynamic gallery of the properties you've sold there. Each property in the gallery must link back to its permanent sold listing page, creating a powerful internal linking structure that search engines love. ### Step 3: Enhance with Rich Content and Schema Markup Beyond the basic data, add unique content to each sold page. This could be a client testimonial, a short video walkthrough, or a paragraph detailing the specific marketing strategy used to achieve the sale. This unique content prevents your pages from being seen as thin or duplicative. The real technical power-up comes from implementing `RealEstateListing` schema on each page. Critically, you must set the `status` property to `"https://schema.org/Sold"` and include the `price` and `address` details. This explicitly tells search engines what this page is about, making it prime for rich results and positioning it as a citable source for AI-driven search. This is a core part of the [Canonical Entity Protocol](https://deancacioppo.com/canonical-entity-protocol-unify-your-mls-crm-web/uncategorized/) that unifies your data across platforms. ### Dean's Insider Edge: Leveraging MLS & IDX Policy Knowledge My work on MLS governance and IDX policy committees provides a unique advantage here.
