How to Audit Your Brand’s Visibility Across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Bing, and Google

# How to Audit Brand Visibility Across AI Search and Google: A Practical Guide ## How to Audit Your Brand’s Visibility Across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Bing, and Google To audit brand visibility, test the same branded and non-branded queries in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Bing, and Google, then record mentions, citations, competitors, and local details. Repeat the checks over time: one result is a snapshot, not a promise of future visibility. Google rankings still matter, but they no longer show the whole picture. Customers may encounter a brand in a traditional search result, an AI-generated answer, or a citation beneath a response. Those appearances can differ by query, platform, location, and date. For agencies, this changes the reporting question. It is no longer enough to ask whether a client ranks on Google. You also need to know whether answer engines mention the business, what evidence they use, and which competitors appear instead. Use recurring gaps to guide useful content, distribution, and follow-up measurement; treat every audit as a dated snapshot rather than a prediction. ## How Can You Check Whether AI Answers and Search Results Show Your Brand? A useful visibility audit compares the same set of queries across platforms and records whether your brand appears, how it appears, and what sources support the result. Keeping the test consistent makes it easier to spot patterns without mistaking a one-off response for a dependable trend. ### Build a query set Start with a small set of queries that reflects how people discover, evaluate, and choose the business. Include branded searches, category comparisons, location intent, and questions about the problem a service solves. For example, an agency auditing a plumbing company might test: - **Branded:** “What does Northstar Plumbing offer?” - **Category:** “Best plumbing service in Manchester” - **Local intent:** “Who provides emergency plumbing near Ancoats?” - **Problem-solving:** “How do I choose a reliable plumber in Manchester?” These examples use a real city and neighborhood to demonstrate query structure; substitute the client’s actual service area when running an audit. Avoid making every test a branded query. People who have not heard of a business are more likely to search by service, need, or location. Keep the query set stable between audit rounds. If you change wording, platform, or location at the same time, you may not know which change caused a different result. ### Test each platform consistently Run the queries in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Bing, and Google, checking AI-generated results where they are available. Use the same wording and location across platforms, and record the date and any relevant context, such as whether you were signed in. Search responses may vary over time and according to the platform’s features or context. For that reason, a single test should not be treated as proof that a brand is always present—or always absent. Recheck important queries periodically, using the same process so the results remain comparable. Google Search Console can help you understand how a website performs in Google Search, but a Google-only report does not capture the full set of places customers may encounter a brand. A broader audit treats Google as one important channel within a wider search and answer ecosystem. ### Record what appears For each query, note whether the brand is mentioned, linked, recommended, and described accurately. A name appearing without a relevant link or useful explanation is different from a clear citation that supports the answer. Track the competitors named and the sources referenced by the platform. If the answer recommends a rival, note whether the competitor has stronger service information, location evidence, third-party references, or clearer pages answering the query. Do not assume you know why a platform chose one source; record what you can observe. For local-intent queries, check whether the response names the correct service area, address, neighborhood, or other location detail. A mention that attaches a business to the wrong location can be more concerning than no mention at all. **Brand mention**: The platform names the business in its answer. **Citation**: The platform provides a source or link that supports a claim or points to relevant information. **Recommendation**: The answer presents the business as a relevant option for the user’s request. **Accuracy**: The answer’s description of the business, its services, and its coverage matches the information the business provides. ## How Do You Measure Local Visibility and Find the Gaps? Local visibility depends on whether a platform can connect the right brand to the right service, location, and evidence—not simply whether a website ranks. A scorecard makes those connections visible and gives the agency a practical way to prioritize improvements. ### Create a platform-by-location scorecard Organize the audit by query, platform, and target service area. For each result, mark whether the brand was mentioned, cited, accurately described, or absent. Add a note about competitors and sources so the scorecard captures context instead of reducing everything to a single number. | Query and location | Platform | Mentioned | Cited | Accurate | Competitors or sources | |---|---|---|---|---|---| | Service query for target area | ChatGPT | Yes / No | Yes / No | Yes / No | Names and links shown | | Service query for target area | Perplexity | Yes / No | Yes / No | Yes / No | Names and links shown | | Service query for target area | Bing | Yes / No | Yes / No | Yes / No | Names and links shown | | Service query for target area | Google | Yes / No | Yes / No | Yes / No | Results and sources shown | Use clear labels rather than guessing at a platform’s internal reasoning. The scorecard is an observation tool, not a measure of what an AI system has learned or will show next. Compare results across locations as well as platforms. A business might be visible for a central district but absent for a nearby service area, or be named by one platform while a competitor dominates another. Those differences can reveal where local evidence or service information is unclear. ### Check the evidence behind local claims Review the pages and business information that support the brand’s local relevance. Check location and service-area pages, business listings, reviews, and consistency in core business details. Look for outdated coverage, conflicting addresses, unclear service descriptions, or claims that are not supported elsewhere. A useful location page should explain what the business does in that area, what services it provides, and what relevant evidence a prospective customer can review. It should answer a real local need—not simply repeat a city name across near-identical pages. This is where an agency’s content strategy needs judgment, not just production volume. A page with a clear purpose and helpful local context gives both customers and search systems more useful information than a thin page created to target another place name. ### Prioritize fixes by intent Start with service-and-location combinations that matter to the client’s customers. Improve the pages that should answer those queries, clarify service coverage, and provide useful evidence. Add structured information where appropriate, but do not treat structured data as a guarantee that a platform will cite or recommend a page. If the audit reveals that a brand is not showing for a problem-solving query, the fix may be a page that answers that question directly. If a platform mentions the brand but gets its coverage wrong, review the business’s location information and service-area language. If competitors appear more often, examine what their pages make clearer—not just how many pages they have. Measure changes over time. Publishing a new page or correcting a listing may improve the quality of available information, but no single edit can guarantee an immediate change in a search result or AI answer. ## How Can Agencies Turn the Audit Into a Repeatable Visibility Workflow? Agencies can make visibility audits actionable by connecting query tracking to content improvements, publishing, distribution, and regular measurement. The workflow matters because a spreadsheet full of findings does not help a client until someone turns those findings into decisions and completed work. ### Translate findings into a content plan Map each recurring gap to a page, audience, location, and search intent. Identify whether the work calls for updating an existing page, creating a genuinely useful local resource, improving business information, or distributing content through an established workflow. Prioritize meaningful service-and-location combinations over repetitive city pages. Assign an owner and deadline to every action, then record what changed and when. This connects the audit to work the agency can review with the client rather than leaving recommendations buried in a report.
