The Traditional 6% Commission Is No Longer the Only Path for Northshore Sellers.

How the 6% Commission Works in the Mandeville & Covington Market

The standard 6% real estate commission is no longer the only option for Northshore sellers looking to maximize their home's equity. For decades, this fee has been the unquestioned industry standard. It's typically split down the middle: 3% for the listing agent's brokerage (the agent who represents you) and 3% for the buyer's agent's brokerage (the agent who brings the buyer). This structure was established long before the internet, Zillow, and digital marketing transformed how homes are bought and sold. Today, technology allows for incredible efficiency in marketing and managing a sale, making the 6% model an outdated and unnecessarily expensive way to transact real estate. Exploring Alternatives Can Be Risky Without the Right Local Expertise.

The For-Sale-By-Owner (FSBO) Gamble in Louisiana

While FSBO and Flat-Fee models offer potential savings, they often require Northshore sellers to sacrifice expert guidance, marketing reach, and negotiation power. The primary allure of the For-Sale-By-Owner route is obvious: saving the entire listing commission. However, this path is fraught with financial and legal risks that can quickly erase any potential savings.
  • Pricing Errors: The Northshore real estate market is nuanced. Overpricing your home in a competitive Mandeville school district can cause it to sit for months, while underpricing a unique historic home in Covington could mean leaving tens of thousands of dollars on the table. Without access to real-time MLS comparable data, FSBO sellers are often guessing at their most valuable asset's price.
  • Marketing Blind Spots: Sticking a sign in the yard and posting on social media is not a marketing strategy. The vast majority of qualified buyers are working with Realtors who search the Multiple Listing Service (MLS). Without an MLS listing, your home is invisible to the largest pool of potential buyers.
  • Legal Liability: Louisiana has specific legal requirements for sellers, including the mandatory Residential Property Disclosure Form. An error or omission on this document can lead to serious legal and financial repercussions after the sale. Navigating purchase agreements, addendums, and inspection responses without an expert is a significant gamble.
  • Lower Net Proceeds: The most compelling argument against FSBO comes from the data. According to the National Association of REALTORS® 2023 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, the median sales price for a FSBO home was $310,000, compared to $405,000 for agent-assisted sales. This gap often exceeds the commission a seller was trying to save in the first place.

The Flat-Fee MLS "Solution": A Half-Measure

The flat-fee model seems like a good compromise. For a few hundred dollars, a company will place your home on the local MLS, giving you the exposure you lack with a pure FSBO. While it's a step up, it's ultimately an incomplete, "à la carte" solution that leaves you to do the most difficult work of a real estate agent. Once your home is listed, you are entirely on your own. You are responsible for hiring and paying for a professional photographer, writing compelling listing descriptions, scheduling and managing every showing, vetting potential buyers for financial qualification, and handling all communication. Most importantly, when an offer comes in, you have no professional advocate to negotiate price, terms, inspection repairs, or appraisal issues. The flat-fee broker’s job is done once the listing is live, leaving you to navigate the most critical and high-stakes part of the transaction alone.
