Louisiana Insurers Are Dropping Homes with Outdated Insulation Due to Increased Storm and Moisture Risk.

This isn't about you; it's about the materials in your attic. After years of massive storm-related payouts, insurance companies are aggressively cutting risk. They now see old, water-absorbent insulation not as a feature, but as a liability that can lead to catastrophic claims from mold, wood rot, and roof failure during a hurricane.

The Hidden Failures of Old Insulation

The Mold & Moisture Threat: Old fiberglass batts and cellulose act like sponges in New Orleans' humid climate. They trap moisture against your wood framing, creating a perfect breeding ground for toxic mold and causing the slow, silent wood rot that insurers fear. This leads to costly moisture remediation and structural repairs that are entirely preventable.

Structural Weakness During Hurricanes: Outdated insulation offers zero structural support. During high winds, it does nothing to prevent roof uplift. Insurers know that a roof deck secured with closed-cell spray foam is significantly more resistant to being torn off in a storm, effectively gluing the sheathing to the rafters.

The Red Flag of a Failed Four-Point Inspection: Home inspectors are now specifically trained to identify compressed, water-stained, or pest-infested insulation as a major red flag. This happens during a four-point inspection covering the Roof, HVAC, Electrical, and Plumbing. A failing grade on the "roof/attic" portion can trigger an immediate policy cancellation, which is why you should never skip a home inspection.

Upgrading to Modern Spray Foam or Blown-In Insulation Can Make Your Home Insurable Again.

The solution is to replace the liability in your attic with an asset. Modern insulation systems, professionally installed, directly address the core concerns of insurance underwriters. They create a sealed, dry, and structurally reinforced building envelope that performs reliably in Louisiana's harsh climate.

How Modern Insulation Passes the Test

Spray Foam: The Air & Moisture Barrier: Closed-cell spray foam creates a monolithic, waterproof, and airtight seal. It stops humid air and moisture from entering the attic, eliminating the risk of condensation and mold growth that insurers penalize. This is the gold standard for homes in high-humidity zones like Metairie, Slidell, and Mandeville.

Blown-In Insulation: A High-Performance Alternative: Modern, high-performance blown-in insulation is a significant upgrade over old materials. When properly installed to the right depth and density, it provides a superior thermal barrier. Unlike old batts, it fills every crevice to prevent air leaks.

Passing the Inspection and Lowering Premiums: A properly insulated and air-sealed attic gets a passing grade on inspections. It demonstrates to the insurer that you have proactively mitigated risks of water damage and roof failure. This can not only save your policy but may also qualify you for discounts.
