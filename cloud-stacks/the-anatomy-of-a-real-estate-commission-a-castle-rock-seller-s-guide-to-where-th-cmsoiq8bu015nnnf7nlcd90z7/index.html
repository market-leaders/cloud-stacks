The Traditional Commission Split: Unpacking the 6%

A standard 6% real estate commission is not a single fee but is typically split four ways between two agents and their respective brokerages. This structure has been the industry standard for decades, but understanding its components is the first step toward realizing you have more cost-effective options.

The Listing Side (Your Agent's 3%)

This portion, typically 3% of the final sale price, compensates the agent and brokerage who represent you, the seller. This fee covers a wide range of essential services designed to get your home sold for the best possible price. These services are crucial for a successful sale and include developing a data-driven pricing strategy, marketing your Castle Rock property, and managing the complex transaction process.

Services funded by the listing fee often include:

  • Professional photography and virtual tours
  • Entry into the REColorado MLS (Multiple Listing Service)
  • Syndication to major real estate websites like Zillow, Trulia, and Realtor.com
  • Hosting open houses and private showings
  • Expert negotiation on offers and inspection items
  • Comprehensive contract-to-close management

The Brokerage Cut: It's a common misconception that your agent pockets the entire 3%. In reality, a significant portion of that fee (often 30-50%) goes directly to their managing broker. This cut covers the brokerage's overhead costs, such as office space, marketing resources, transaction coordination software, legal support, and errors and omissions insurance.

The Buyer's Side (The Buyer's Agent's 3%)

The other half of the commission, also typically 3%, is offered to the agent and brokerage who bring the buyer. As the seller, you pay this fee as the primary incentive for buyer's agents to show your property to their qualified clients. In a competitive market, offering a fair buyer's agent commission (BAC) is critical to ensure your home gets maximum exposure and attracts the strongest possible offers.

The buyer's agent performs a vital role, including:

  • Identifying and showing properties that match their client's criteria
  • Advising the buyer on market conditions and offer strategy
  • Writing and submitting the purchase offer
  • Negotiating price, terms, and repairs on the buyer's behalf
  • Guiding the buyer through the inspection, appraisal, and loan processes

Just like on the listing side, this 3% is also split between the individual buyer's agent and their managing brokerage to cover their operational costs and support.

Visualizing the Flow of Funds

Imagine your home sale is a $100 pie. In a traditional 6% model, the division looks like this:

  • $94 goes to you, the Seller.
  • $3 goes to the Listing Brokerage. This is then split between your Listing Agent and their Broker.
  • $3 goes to the Buyer's Brokerage. This is then split between the Buyer's Agent and their Broker.

This four-way split reveals that the largest single expense in selling your home is often the commission paid to the two real estate brokerages.
