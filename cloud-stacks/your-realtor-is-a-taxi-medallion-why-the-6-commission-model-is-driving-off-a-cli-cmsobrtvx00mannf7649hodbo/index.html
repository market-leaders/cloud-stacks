The Taxi Medallion & The 6% Commission: A Story of Gatekeeping

The taxi medallion system worked because it created artificial scarcity. A limited number of medallions meant a limited number of cabs, giving medallion owners total control over the market and pricing. For decades, the real estate industry operated on a similar principle. The local Multiple Listing Service (MLS) was a closed-off database, and only a licensed agent could access it. They held the "keys to the kingdom," controlling the flow of information about what homes were for sale, for how much, and to whom. In that world, a 6% commission made some sense. Agents were the sole source of market data and the only channel to a wide pool of buyers. They spent immense time and money on print advertising, manual paperwork, and physically networking to connect sellers with buyers. The fee was a payment for access and manual labor—a toll for using the exclusive information highway they controlled.
