An Assignment of Benefits (AOB) Gives a Contractor Total Control of Your Insurance Claim

An Assignment of Benefits is a legal agreement that transfers your insurance policy rights to a contractor, giving them the power to manage your claim and collect payment directly from your insurer. While it might be presented as a convenient, hands-off solution, it opens the door to significant financial and legal problems for homeowners across Louisiana and Mississippi.

What Exactly is an Assignment of Benefits?

Assignment of Benefits (AOB): A legal contract that transfers the insurance policy benefits and rights from you (the policyholder) to a third party (the roofing contractor). This means the contractor can bill your insurer, collect payment directly, and even file a lawsuit against your insurance company in your name—all without your direct involvement or approval.

The Red Flag: Why Storm Chasers Love AOBs

AOBs are the primary tool of out-of-town storm chasers who flood areas from the North Shore of Lake Pontchartrain to the Mississippi Gulf Coast after a hurricane. These contractors use high-pressure sales tactics, promise a "free roof" (which is insurance fraud), and rush you to sign a digital form before you can read the fine print. Once they have your signature and the insurance check, they often disappear, leaving you with poor workmanship and no one to call for warranty issues.

The Financial Dangers: Inflated Claims, Liens, and Lawsuits

For the financial-minded homeowner, the risks are severe. A contractor with an AOB can inflate the claim far beyond the actual cost of repairs. If your insurance company disputes the inflated amount, the contractor can place a mechanic's lien on your property for the unpaid balance. This means you can't sell or refinance your home until the lien is paid. The contractor could also sue the insurer in your name, delaying your roof repair indefinitely and leaving you caught in a legal battle.

What to Do Immediately After a Storm (Before You Sign Anything)

  1. Ensure Safety. Before anything else, check for immediate hazards like downed power lines or unstable structures.
  2. Document Everything. Take photos and videos of the damage from the ground. This initial documentation is crucial for your claim.
  3. Mitigate Further Damage. If you have active leaks, call a professional for emergency roof repair and tarping to prevent further interior damage.
  4. Call Your Insurer. Let them know you have damage and will be getting an estimate from a trusted local contractor before making any decisions.
