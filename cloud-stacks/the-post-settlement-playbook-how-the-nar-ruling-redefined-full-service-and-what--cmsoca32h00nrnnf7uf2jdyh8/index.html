What Actually Changed? Uncoupling Commissions Explained

For decades, the system for paying real estate agents has been relatively standard across the country, including right here in St. Tammany Parish. This recent settlement throws that old model out the window.

The Old Way: Traditionally, a home seller agreed to pay a total commission, often around 6% of the sale price. This fee was entered into the local MLS, like our Gulf South Real Estate Information Network (GSREIN), with a pre-determined portion automatically offered to the agent who brought the buyer. This "cooperative compensation" was the bedrock of the old system.

The New Way (Post-Settlement): Now, the mandatory offer of cooperative compensation through the MLS is gone. As a seller, you will still negotiate a listing fee with your agent. However, the amount a buyer's agent will be paid is no longer advertised on the MLS and becomes a separate point of negotiation, primarily between the buyer and their own agent. This creates a more transparent, consumer-driven marketplace.

The Bottom Line for You: This shift means you now have a direct line of sight into exactly what you are paying for. It’s a powerful opportunity to question every dollar spent to sell your home and ensure you’re receiving true value, a core principle of the current real estate market.
