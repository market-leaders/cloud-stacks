The Problem - Understanding the Attack on Your Roof

Salt air corrosion silently degrades Mississippi Gulf Coast roofs, leading to premature failure and costly, unexpected repairs.

The constant exposure to salt-laden air creates a highly corrosive environment that typical roofing materials are not designed to withstand. This leads to a gradual breakdown of your roof's most critical components, often going unnoticed until a major leak or storm reveals the damage.

How Salt Air Acts Like Acid on Your Roof

The science is straightforward: salt, when combined with moisture and oxygen, creates an electrochemical reaction that dramatically accelerates rust and corrosion. Here in Mississippi, the high humidity supercharges this process, meaning your roof is under attack 24/7. It's a slow, invisible process that eats away at metal, turning strong components brittle and weak long before their expected lifespan is up.

The Most Vulnerable Parts of Your Roof System

Corrosion doesn't attack everything at once. It targets the metal weak points in your roofing system, which can compromise the entire structure. The first components to fail are often:

  • Metal Flashing: The seals around chimneys, vents, and skylights are critical. Once corroded flashing fails, water has a direct path into your attic.
  • Nails & Fasteners: These are the glue holding your shingles down. Rusted fasteners lose their grip, allowing shingles to lift and blow off in a storm, a common sign of wind damage.
  • Drip Edges & Gutters: As the first line of defense for your roof decking and fascia, corroded drip edges can lead to wood rot and costly structural repairs.
  • Metal Roof Panels: If you have a metal roof, the type of metal and its protective coating are the only things standing between the salt air and total failure.

Warning Signs You Can Spot from the Ground

You don't need to get on a ladder to spot the early signs of corrosion. Empower yourself by looking for these red flags:

  • Reddish-brown rust stains on shingles or siding, especially below vents or chimneys.
  • Bubbling, flaking, or peeling paint on any metal roof components.
  • Loose or lifted shingles, particularly after minor wind events, which can indicate fastener failure.
