Why That "Bargain" Bid Is So Low (And What You're *Really* Paying For)

A bargain roofing bid often hides the true costs of subpar materials and unskilled labor that will fail under Gulf South conditions. That unbelievably low price isn’t due to efficiency; it’s due to dangerous compromises. Here’s the breakdown of what’s being cut from a cheap bid and why it matters in our region.

Inferior Materials Not Rated for Louisiana Heat & Mississippi Humidity

The contractor is using architectural shingles with poor granule adhesion, thinner underlayment that tears easily, and cheap, rust-prone flashing. Our relentless sun bakes low-quality shingles, causing them to become brittle and crack in just a few years. High humidity gets trapped by improper roof ventilation and cheap underlayment, leading to deck rot from the inside out.

Cut-Corner Installation by Inexperienced Crews

Unskilled laborers, often paid per square, rush the job. They use nail guns with the pressure set too high (blow-through), misalign shingles, and use caulk to seal critical flashing points instead of installing it correctly. Improperly nailed shingles are the first to fly off in a tropical storm, and poorly installed flashing around chimneys and vents guarantees a leak during our next heavy downpour.

The "Storm Chaser" Business Model: Here Today, Gone Tomorrow

Out-of-state companies flood areas like the North Shore or the Mississippi Gulf Coast after a storm. They have no local office, no ties to the community, and no intention of sticking around. Their "warranty" is worthless because their phone number will be disconnected by the time your roof starts leaking a year later, leaving you with no recourse.

The Disastrous Aftermath: When a Cheap Roof Fails

Choosing a cheap roofer in the Gulf South means you're paying for future leaks, denied insurance claims, and a worthless warranty. The initial savings are quickly erased by a cascade of expensive, stressful problems. The failure of a cheap roof isn't a matter of if, but when.

From a Small Drip to Catastrophic Interior Damage

A single installation error creates a pathway for water. That water intrusion leads to stained ceilings, ruined drywall, and worst of all, the rapid growth of mold in our humid climate—a serious health hazard and an expensive remediation project. What starts as a small stain can quickly escalate into thousands of dollars in interior repairs.

The Local Code Violation Trap in Coastal Louisiana & Mississippi

Bargain contractors often ignore local building codes that are specifically designed for our High-Wind Zone. Municipalities from Baton Rouge and the River Parishes to Gulfport and Biloxi, MS, have stringent roofing codes for a reason. A non-compliant roof can result in a failed inspection when you try to sell your home, and your insurance company can use code violations as a reason to deny your claim after the next hurricane.

Voided Manufacturer Warranties & Insurance Nightmares

Shingle manufacturers have strict installation requirements, and a cheap roofer's methods will almost certainly void that 30-year material warranty. When your "new" roof fails after three years, you have no warranty from the manufacturer and no warranty from the contractor who installed it. You are left completely unprotected, facing the full cost of another replacement.
