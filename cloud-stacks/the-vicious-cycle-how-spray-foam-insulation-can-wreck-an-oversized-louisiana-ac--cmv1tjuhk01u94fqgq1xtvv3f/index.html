The Problem: The Vicious Cycle Explained

An oversized AC unit in a newly insulated home will short-cycle, failing to remove Louisiana's oppressive humidity. This isn't a minor issue; it's the start of a domino effect that compromises your comfort, your home's health, and your air conditioner's lifespan. The investment you made to improve your home is now actively working against you.

What is "Short-Cycling" and Why is it a New Orleans Nightmare?

Short-Cycling: The AC unit turns on, blasts cold air, satisfies the thermostat's temperature setting in just a few minutes, and shuts off. This rapid on-off-on-off sequence is the core of the problem.

The real function of an air conditioner in the Gulf South isn't just cooling; it's dehumidification. This critical process only happens when the system runs for a sustained period, allowing the evaporator coil to get cold enough to condense moisture out of the air. Short cycles cool the air but leave the water vapor behind, creating that signature cold, clammy, and deeply uncomfortable feeling common in New Orleans homes. This lingering moisture can lead to musty smells and create conditions ripe for mildew and mold growth, posing a direct threat to your indoor air quality.

The Silent Killer: How Short-Cycling Destroys Your AC's Compressor

The constant starting and stopping of short-cycling is the mechanical equivalent of driving your car in stop-and-go traffic on the I-10 during rush hour versus cruising on the open highway. The start-up phase puts the most strain on the machinery. Your AC's compressor is its heart and, by far, its most expensive component. Each start-up forces the compressor to overcome immense pressure, causing a surge of electrical current and significant mechanical stress.

When this happens every few minutes instead of a few times an hour, the wear and tear is exponential. The lubrication inside the compressor struggles to circulate properly, leading to overheating and internal damage. This relentless abuse leads to a premature, catastrophic failure of the compressor, often requiring a full replacement of the entire outdoor unit—a massive, unexpected expense that can easily wipe out any savings from your insulation project.

The Insulation Paradox: Why Your Energy Bills Might Not Drop as Expected

You installed spray foam insulation to see your energy bills plummet. But if your AC is short-cycling, you may be disappointed. The power surge during compressor start-up is the most energy-intensive part of an AC's operation. A system that is constantly starting and stopping can use as much, or even more, energy than an older, less efficient unit that runs for longer, steadier cycles.

This is the ultimate pain point. You invested thousands in a premium product like spray foam to save money and improve comfort. Instead, you're still uncomfortable, your energy savings are minimal, and you're now facing a multi-thousand-dollar AC replacement bill. This is the direct result of treating insulation as a standalone product instead of one part of an interconnected home system.

Step 1: It Starts with a Diagnostic—The Blower Door Test

Before we do anything, we need a "before" picture of your home's performance. A blower door test is a diagnostic tool that measures your home's air leakage rate. By depressurizing the house, we can quantify exactly how much air is leaking in from the outside through countless gaps and cracks in the building envelope.

This test provides a hard data baseline. It tells us precisely how "leaky" your home is and allows our certified experts to calculate how much the heat load will be reduced after we install spray foam insulation and perform professional air sealing. This isn't guesswork; it's the foundation of all our recommendations.

Step 2: Calculating the Real "Heat Load" with a Manual J Calculation

Once we know the "after" picture—how airtight your home will be—we can perform a Manual J calculation. This is the industry-standard protocol for determining the exact size and capacity of the air conditioning unit a home truly needs. Most HVAC contractors size units based on square footage alone, a rule of thumb that is wildly inaccurate for a high-performance, air-sealed home.

The Sunlight Contractors difference is that we perform these crucial calculations based on the post-insulation data from the blower door test. This ensures your HVAC system is perfectly matched to your home's new, highly efficient state. This prevents short-cycling, guarantees proper dehumidification, and protects your HVAC investment for years to come.
