What Makes Selling a Home in Covington and Mandeville So Challenging Today?

Selling a home in Covington and Mandeville is challenging due to rising insurance premiums and strict post-hurricane inspection requirements that can derail a sale. These modern hurdles directly threaten the equity you’ve built in your Northshore property. Buyers, facing their own affordability challenges, are more cautious than ever, and a difficult inspection or an uninsurable roof can quickly terminate a contract, leaving you back at square one.

The Insurance Gauntlet: Uninsurable Roofs and Soaring Premiums

The current insurance crisis on the Northshore is a major obstacle for sellers. Driven by significant losses from weather events like wind and hail, which account for a large portion of homeowners insurance claims according to the Insurance Information Institute, insurers are refusing to cover homes with roofs over a certain age, often as young as 10-15 years. This isn't a suggestion; it's a hard stop for most transactions. If a buyer cannot get insurance, they cannot secure a mortgage, effectively making your home unsellable without a costly roof replacement.

Inspection Report Nightmares: The High Cost of Repairs

Home inspectors across St. Tammany Parish are now more meticulous than ever, flagging everything from minor electrical issues to potential foundation concerns. While thoroughness is good, it provides buyers with a long list of demands. They often use these reports to request thousands of dollars in repairs or credits at the closing table, directly reducing your net profit from the sale and complicating the local real estate market dynamics.

How These Hurdles Impact Your Bottom Line

These two factors combine to create a perfect storm for sellers on the Northshore. You are often forced into a difficult choice: either pay for major repairs like a new roof upfront or give up a significant portion of your sale price in concessions. In either scenario, the final payout you receive is substantially smaller than you anticipated, shrinking the equity you worked for years to build.
