How the 6% Commission is Typically Split in a Georgia Transaction

The 6% figure isn't a single payment to one person; it's divided among multiple parties involved in the transaction. The process can seem opaque, but it generally follows a clear path. The total commission is first split down the middle, with 3% allocated to the seller's side (the listing brokerage) and 3% allocated to the buyer's side (the buyer's brokerage).

From there, each of those 3% portions is split again. The listing agent receives a portion of their 3%, and their managing broker receives the rest. Similarly, the buyer's agent shares their 3% with their managing broker. This four-way split covers marketing costs, brokerage overhead, insurance, and the agents' income, but it also highlights how many hands are in the pot, diminishing the net proceeds for the person whose asset is actually being sold—the homeowner.

The "Why" Behind the Traditional 6% Model

To understand why 6% became the standard, we have to look back to a pre-internet world. Before the digital age, real estate agents were the sole gatekeepers of property information. The Multiple Listing Service (MLS) was a closed system, accessible only to licensed professionals. If you wanted to sell your home, you needed an agent to get it in front of other agents and their buyers.

Marketing was an expensive, labor-intensive process. It involved costly print advertisements in newspapers like The Atlanta Journal-Constitution, creating and distributing physical flyers, and significant time spent networking through phone calls and office meetings. The high overhead and the agent's exclusive access to information justified a higher commission rate. It was a system that made sense for its time, but it has become profoundly outdated by technological efficiency and the free flow of information.
