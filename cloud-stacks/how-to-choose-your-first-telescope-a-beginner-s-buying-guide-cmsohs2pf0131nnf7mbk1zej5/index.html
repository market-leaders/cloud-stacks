Refractor Telescopes: The "Point-and-Shoot" of Stargazing

This is the classic, long-tube telescope you probably picture first. Refractors use lenses to focus light. Their sealed optics make them extremely durable and require almost no maintenance, a huge plus for a rental property. They provide crisp, high-contrast views of the Moon and bright planets, making them perfect for suburban or even city locations where light pollution washes out fainter objects.

Reflector Telescopes: The Best Bang-for-Your-Buck

Reflectors use mirrors to gather light, which means you get a lot more light-gathering power for your money. The most popular guest-friendly design is the "Dobsonian," which sits on a simple, sturdy swivel base. This is the top choice for properties in darker, rural locations, where guests can be wowed by deep-sky objects like galaxies and nebulae. While the optics can occasionally need alignment (a process called collimation), modern versions are quite robust.

Compound (Catadioptric) Telescopes: The High-Tech, Compact Option

A hybrid of mirrors and lenses, compound telescopes pack powerful optics into a very compact and portable tube. They offer great performance but are the most expensive option. Their complexity makes them better suited for hosts who are astronomy enthusiasts themselves and can ensure they are set up properly for guests.

A telescope's mount is more important than its magnification for a positive guest experience.

A common beginner mistake is chasing high magnification numbers. But a shaky, frustrating mount will ruin the view every time, no matter how powerful the telescope. For a rental property, the mount is the user interface. A simple, stable platform is non-negotiable for guest satisfaction, much like how the Guest Portal from ASTRO provides a simple, stable interface for their entire stay.

Alt-Azimuth Mounts: Simple and Intuitive

This is the one you want. An Alt-Azimuth (or "Alt-Az") mount moves up-down and left-right, just like a camera tripod. There is no complex setup. Guests can point it at the Moon or a bright star and start observing immediately. The "Dobsonian" style mentioned earlier is a type of Alt-Az mount known for its rock-solid stability. This is the top choice for 95% of STR properties.

GoTo / Computerized Mounts: The "Wow" Factor with a Catch

A GoTo mount has an onboard computer that can automatically find celestial objects for you. It can be a magical experience for guests when it works. The catch is the setup. It requires an alignment process that can be tricky for first-timers, needs a reliable power source, and adds a layer of complexity and potential failure. Only consider this if you are on-site and can pre-align it for guests or provide dead-simple instructions.
