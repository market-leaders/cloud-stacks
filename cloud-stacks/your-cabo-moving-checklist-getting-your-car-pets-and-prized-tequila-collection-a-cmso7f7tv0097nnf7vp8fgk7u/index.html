Section 1: Your Vehicle

Bringing your American car to Cabo requires a specific Temporary Import Permit (TIP) for legal driving in Mexico.

Even though the entire Baja California peninsula is considered a "hassle-free zone," securing the right permit is crucial for legal compliance, insurance purposes, and peace of mind as you settle into your new home in Los Cabos.

What is a Temporary Vehicle Import Permit (TIP)?

Temporary Vehicle Import Permit (TIP): Known in Spanish as the Permiso de Importación Temporal de Vehículos, this is the official document that allows a foreign-plated car to be legally driven in Mexico outside the designated border zones for an extended period. It's tied directly to your immigration status, whether you're on a visitor permit (FMM) or have a temporary/permanent resident visa.

Step-by-Step: Getting Your TIP for Baja California Sur

You have two main paths to get this essential document. The seasoned experts at Coastal Properties of Cabo have guided countless clients through this process, and we recommend starting online.

  • Option 1: Online via Banjercito. This is the most efficient method. You can apply online through the Banjercito (the official Mexican military bank that handles these permits) website 7 to 60 days before your trip. You'll need digital copies of your passport, driver's license, and vehicle title or registration. Once approved, print the receipt and permit to bring with you.
  • Option 2: At the Border. You can also get your permit in person at a CIITEV module at the border crossing. Be prepared for potential lines and have all your original documents ready.

Expect to pay a permit fee of around $50-$60 USD, plus a refundable deposit that varies based on your vehicle's year. This deposit is returned when you properly cancel the permit upon leaving Mexico.

Insurance: Why Your US Policy Isn't Enough

This is non-negotiable: your American auto insurance policy is not valid in Mexico. You are legally required to have liability insurance from a Mexican company. For anyone moving to Cabo San Lucas with their primary vehicle, we strongly recommend getting a full-coverage policy for complete protection.

Section 2: Your Four-Legged Family Members

Moving your pets to Cabo San Lucas is straightforward with the correct, up-to-date veterinary health certificate.

Mexico is incredibly welcoming to pets, but the key to a smooth entry at the San José del Cabo International Airport (SJD) or a land border crossing is having your paperwork in perfect order.

The Essential Document: The APHIS Health Certificate

The one document you absolutely must have is an official health certificate. It must be issued by a USDA-accredited veterinarian, and while not required, a bilingual format is always a helpful touch. The most critical part is the timing: the certificate must be issued within 10 days of your travel date. It needs to state your pet is healthy, has a valid rabies vaccination (if applicable), and is free of parasites.

Flying vs. Driving with Your Pet to Los Cabos

Deciding how to get your furry friend to your new Cabo San Lucas oceanfront real estate depends on their temperament and size. When flying, you'll need to check each airline's specific rules on carrier size and book their spot well in advance. Upon landing at SJD, you'll visit the SENASICA/OISA office for a quick inspection of your pet and their paperwork. Driving can be less stressful for some animals, just be sure to keep their documents handy for any border or military checkpoints along Highway 1.

Life in Cabo with Your Pet

Once you're here, you'll find Los Cabos is a paradise for pets, too. From pet-friendly beaches in communities like Zacatitos to excellent local vets and groomers in San Jose del Cabo, your pet will love the sunny lifestyle as much as you do.

Section 3: Your Prized Possessions (Including the Tequila)

Your prized tequila collection can cross the border, but you must adhere to Mexico's customs (Aduana) limits to avoid high taxes.

Moving your household goods, from furniture to fine art, is a common part of setting up your new life. Understanding the rules of declaration and duty is essential for a smooth transition into your Cabo luxury home.

Understanding Your Duty-Free Alcohol Allowance

Let's get straight to the good stuff. Per Mexican customs regulations, each adult is typically allowed to bring in 3 liters of liquor and 6 liters of wine duty-free. It's always wise to check the official Aduana (Mexican Customs) website before you travel, as these limits can change. If you declare more than the allowance, you won't lose your collection, but you will have to pay import duties and taxes on the excess.

Beyond Tequila: Moving Your Household Goods

For a full-scale move, you'll want to know about the Menaje de Casa. This is a one-time, duty-free importation of your used household goods. It is available only to those who have already secured a Temporary or Permanent Resident visa. You must get this permit from a Mexican Consulate in the US *before* you move your items. For this process, using a reputable cross-border moving company that understands the paperwork is worth its weight in gold.
