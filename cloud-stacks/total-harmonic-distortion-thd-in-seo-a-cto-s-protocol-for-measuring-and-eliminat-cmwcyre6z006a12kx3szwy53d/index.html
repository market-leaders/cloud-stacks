Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) in SEO is the measure of all semantic noise—contradictory data, vague content, and structural inconsistencies—that degrades your website's core signal to search engines. It’s the digital friction preventing you from ranking. A pure signal is a website where every single data point, from the schema in the header to the address in the footer, is perfectly consistent and reinforces a single, authoritative entity. High THD is a site where the phone number in a blog post from 2015 contradicts the one on your contact page, creating a micro-fracture in your authority that Google’s crawlers detect and penalize. ### Signal vs. Noise in Practice * **Pure Signal (Low THD):** A roofer's website with a dedicated page for "Asphalt Shingle Repair in Denver, CO," which internally links to a parent page about "Denver Roofing Services" and includes structured data specifying the exact service area by zip code. * **Semantic Noise (High THD):** The same roofer has three different pages vaguely titled "Roofing," "Our Services," and "Denver Roofer," all competing with each other and offering no clear, hierarchical information to the crawler. ## How does semantic noise hurt my local business rankings? Semantic noise directly hurts local rankings by creating ambiguity about your business's location, services, and authority, causing Google to favor competitors with a clearer signal. For a plumber, a doctor, or a real estate agent, location and service are not keywords; they are canonical facts. When those facts are distorted, your relevance collapses. Google will not recommend a professional service if it has even a 0.1% doubt about its location or qualifications. ### Real-World Examples of High THD for Local Businesses 1. **The Plumber with Multiple Numbers:** Your Google Business Profile lists one phone number, your homepage another, and a third-party directory a third.
