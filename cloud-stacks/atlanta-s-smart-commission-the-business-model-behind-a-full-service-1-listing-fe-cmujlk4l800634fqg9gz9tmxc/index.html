The Traditional Atlanta Commission Structure

Most Atlanta homeowners pay around 5-6% in total real estate commissions, which is typically split between the seller's agent and the buyer's agent.

This long-standing model has been the primary way real estate is transacted in Georgia, but understanding the breakdown reveals exactly where your money goes and why there's room for a smarter approach. For years, this structure has been the unquestioned norm, but the recent NAR settlement has brought commission structures into the spotlight, empowering sellers to seek more favorable terms.

How the 6% Commission is Divided in a Typical Metro Atlanta Sale

The total commission is not a single fee paid to one person. Instead, it's divided between the two brokerages involved in the transaction. Typically, 3% is allocated to the listing agent's brokerage for marketing and selling the home, and 3% is offered to the buyer's agent's brokerage for bringing a qualified buyer to the table.

For example, on a $500,000 home sale in an Atlanta suburb like Alpharetta or a property in Gwinnett County, this amounts to $30,000 in total commissions. That breaks down to $15,000 for the listing side and $15,000 for the buyer's side. This entire amount is paid by the seller from their proceeds at closing.

The "Why" Behind Traditional Fees and What They Cover

Historically, this 6% structure was justified by the significant costs and labor involved in selling a home before the digital age. Agents covered hefty marketing expenses (print ads, flyers), brokerage overhead (large physical offices), insurance, and time-intensive lead generation. This model was built for a pre-internet era and carries significant legacy costs that are no longer necessary for effective marketing.

Today, with the power of the internet, Multiple Listing Services (FMLS and Georgia MLS), and advanced AI and Proptech, the process is far more efficient. Yet, the commission structure has largely remained the same, forcing sellers to pay for outdated operational models.

The Direct Impact on Your Net Proceeds as an Atlanta Seller
