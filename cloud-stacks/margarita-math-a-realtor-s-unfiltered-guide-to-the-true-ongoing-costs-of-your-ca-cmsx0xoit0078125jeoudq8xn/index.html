The Closing Costs & Initial Setup (The First Round)

Your one-time closing costs in Mexico will typically range from 3% to 8% of the total sale price. This initial investment covers the legal and administrative framework for your property ownership as a foreigner, a critical step when buying a home in Cabo San Lucas. Unlike in the U.S., these costs are almost always paid by the buyer and are essential to budget for upfront.

The Fideicomiso (Bank Trust) Setup Fee

For Americans buying property in Los Cabos within the "restricted zone" (50km from the coast), a Fideicomiso is the legal mechanism required for ownership. This is a trust established with a Mexican bank, where the bank holds the title on your behalf, and you are designated as the beneficiary with all the rights of an owner. There is a one-time setup fee paid to the bank to create this legal structure, which is a foundational part of your closing costs. Understanding the nuances between a Fideicomiso vs. a corporation is key, and we can guide you through that choice.

Notary Public Fees & Taxes

The role of the Notario Publico in Mexico is far more significant than that of a notary in the United States. They are highly qualified, government-appointed lawyers who act as a neutral party to ensure the entire transaction is legal, properly recorded, and that all taxes are paid. Their fees cover a comprehensive service that includes drafting the new deed (escritura), verifying the property's legal status, and calculating the taxes. This includes the Acquisition Tax (ISAI - Impuesto Sobre Adquisición de Inmuebles), registration fees for the Public Registry of Property, and various other certificates needed to secure a clean title.

Appraisal, Legal Fees, and Miscellaneous

Before the bank will issue a Fideicomiso, a formal property appraisal, or avalúo, is required to establish the official value for tax purposes. You'll also have fees for your closing attorney or coordinator, who works on your behalf to gather documents and coordinate with the Notary. Finally, there are small but necessary costs for setting up your new utility accounts with CFE (electricity) and the local water authority. These initial steps are part of the essential homeowner checklist for a smooth transition.

The Fixed Annual Costs (The Yearly Tab)

Your largest fixed annual expenses in Cabo are typically your HOA fees and Fideicomiso renewal, not your property taxes. Once you're settled, a predictable set of annual costs will form the baseline of your budget. Understanding these allows for easy financial planning and ensures there are no yearly surprises when the bills arrive. Annual Property Tax (Predial)

Your annual property tax, known as 'Predial,' is significantly lower than what you're used to in the United States. This is one of the most pleasant financial surprises for new owners. The tax is calculated based on the catastral or assessed value, which is typically much lower than the market value of your property. Furthermore, the local government offers attractive discounts, often between 10-20%, for paying the entire year's tax bill early, usually in January or February.

Annual Fideicomiso (Bank Trust) Fee

The Fideicomiso, your bank trust, requires an annual fee to maintain your legal ownership rights as a foreigner. This fee is paid directly to the bank that holds your trust. The cost typically ranges from $450 to $700 USD per year, depending on the bank. It's a small price for the security and legal standing it provides, and keeping this payment current is absolutely critical to protecting your investment.

Homeowner's Association (HOA) Fees

HOA fees, or 'cuotas de mantenimiento,' are often the largest recurring expense for condo and gated community owners in Los Cabos. These fees are the lifeblood of the pristine communities that make living in Los Cabos so appealing. They cover essential services like 24/7 security, immaculate landscaping, resort-style pool maintenance, common area insurance, trash disposal, and general upkeep of the development. For example, monthly fees for a two-bedroom condo in a bustling Cabo San Lucas community might be $400-$600, while a home in an exclusive golf community in San Jose del Cabo could be significantly higher, reflecting a wider array of amenities.

The Variable & Lifestyle Costs (Pay-As-You-Go)

For non-resident owners, budgeting for property management and fluctuating utility bills is the key to protecting your investment. These are the costs that change based on usage, season, and how you choose to manage your property. From keeping the lights on to ensuring your home is secure while you're away, these expenses are critical for a seamless ownership experience, whether you're in a bustling Cabo condo or a quiet home in a community like Zacatitos.

Property Management

Professional property management is a non-negotiable investment for peace of mind and asset protection when you're not in Cabo. For owners who rent out their property, the cost is typically a percentage of rental income, usually between 20-30%. For non-rental properties, a flat monthly fee ranging from $100 to $500+ USD is common, depending on the size of the home and the services required. These services are comprehensive and include paying all utility bills, coordinating maintenance and repairs, conducting regular security checks, and preparing your home for the occasional tropical storm.

Utilities (CFE, Water, Gas, Internet)

Electricity (CFE) in Baja California Sur can be your most significant utility expense, operating on a tiered system where costs rise exponentially with usage. The key is to avoid the high consumption tariff, known as DAC (De Alto Consumo). This requires discipline with air conditioning, which is the primary driver of high bills, especially during the humid summer months. Other utilities include municipal water, propane gas for cooking and water heaters (often delivered by truck), and high-speed internet. In many areas, fiber optic is available, while in more remote locations like the East Cape, Starlink has become a game-changing option.

Insurance & Maintenance

While not always legally required, hurricane and liability insurance is a critical safeguard for your valuable Cabo asset. Given the location, it's vital to secure a policy that specifically covers damage from hurricanes and tropical storms. Beyond insurance, you must budget for routine maintenance to keep your home in top condition. This includes semi-annual A/C servicing, weekly pool care, regular pest control, and upkeep of specialized systems like water purification and softeners, which are common in Los Cabos homes.
