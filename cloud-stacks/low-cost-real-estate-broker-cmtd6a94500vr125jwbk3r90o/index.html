These models are designed to attract clients by focusing exclusively on the fee, but it's crucial to understand what you're getting—and what you're not. ### Flat-Fee vs. Discounted Commission: Understanding the Models There are two main structures for low-fee real estate models that aim to help you save thousands. * **Flat-Fee Broker**: This agent charges a set dollar amount for their services, regardless of the home's final sale price. This fee might be paid upfront or at closing. * **Discounted Commission Broker**: This agent charges a percentage-based commission that is simply lower than the local average (e.g., 1.5% to list instead of the more traditional 2.5-3%). ### The Hidden Trade-Offs: Where Savings Can Become Costs The immediate question for most sellers is, "What's the catch?" The reduction in commission is almost always balanced by a reduction in service. A full-service agent's fee covers a wide array of crucial tasks that a discount model may omit. Common service reductions include limited marketing budgets (no professional photography or virtual tours), minimal negotiation support, or having your transaction managed by a less-experienced junior associate. A poorly marketed home can sit on the market longer, attract lower offers, and ultimately sell for less, erasing any initial commission savings. This is why a discount real estate broker isn't always the same as a full-service experience for less. ### Is a Low-Cost Broker Right for an Experienced Seller?
