The Science of the Concern - Demystifying VOCs and Off-Gassing

The primary health concern with spray foam insulation stems from the chemical reaction during installation, not the final cured product.

This is the fundamental point that gets lost in online debates. The risk isn't from living with the finished insulation; it's from exposure to the chemicals before they have fully and correctly cured into a stable plastic. This is why understanding the science behind spray foam is so critical.

What are VOCs and "Off-Gassing"?

VOCs (Volatile Organic Compounds): These are chemicals that can be released as gases from certain solids or liquids. Many common household products, from paint to cleaning supplies, release VOCs.

Spray foam is created by mixing two liquid components (Isocyanate and Polyol resin). The "off-gassing" of VOCs occurs during the chemical reaction and curing phase as these liquids turn into a solid foam. This off-gassing period is temporary and should be complete within 24-48 hours if the installation is done correctly.

What Happens When Curing Goes Wrong?

If the two chemical components are not mixed at the precise ratio and temperature, the foam may not cure properly. This failure to cure is what leads to lingering chemical smells and prolonged off-gassing, which is the source of nearly all documented health complaints. A common sign of a bad installation is a persistent, unpleasant "fishy smell" that indicates an incomplete chemical reaction.
