The contract itself controls the parties’ rights and deadlines, so read it carefully and ask your broker or appropriate legal professional about anything you do not understand. ## What Happens Between an Accepted Offer and Closing in Indiana? After the seller accepts an offer, both sides work through the contract’s inspection, financing, appraisal, title, and closing requirements. The purchase agreement sets the obligations and deadlines; the timeline is not identical for every transaction. ### Inspection and repair discussions The buyer’s inspection period and available options depend on the contract. An inspection can identify issues that lead to questions, repair requests, or further discussion. The seller should review any request with the broker, understand what the buyer is asking for, and respond within the applicable contract deadlines. Not every inspection finding requires the same response. The parties may negotiate repairs, a credit, another solution, or no change, depending on the contract and the circumstances. A roof concern, for example, may call for a different discussion than a minor maintenance item. Avoid promising work or agreeing to terms before understanding the scope and implications. Inspection results can affect timing if the parties need additional evaluations or amendments. Keep records of agreed work and communicate promptly with the broker. Buyers also have responsibilities during this stage; sellers can learn more about the buyer’s perspective in this guide to [home inspection mistakes to avoid](https://onepercentlistsindiana.com/buy/mistakes-to-avoid-when-getting-a-home-inspection/). ### Appraisal, financing, and title When a buyer uses a mortgage, the lender may order an appraisal as part of its loan process. The lender also completes underwriting, which can involve reviewing financial information and property documentation. The seller may have little direct involvement in those reviews, but quick responses to requests can help prevent avoidable delays. If an appraisal comes in below the contract price, the parties may need to consider their options under the contract and the buyer’s financing situation. The outcome is not automatic; it can involve further negotiation or other steps. See [what sellers can consider when an appraisal comes in low](https://onepercentlistsindiana.com/sell/appraisal-comes-in-low/). ### Final walkthrough and closing day The buyer’s final walkthrough generally takes place before closing, according to the contract and agreed arrangements. It gives the buyer an opportunity to confirm the property’s condition and check that any agreed-upon items have been addressed. Sellers should follow the contract’s move-out and property-condition requirements. On closing day, the closing provider coordinates the signing process and the handling of funds and transfer documents. Timing and procedures depend on the transaction, the closing provider, and the contract. Confirm where and when to sign, what identification or documents are needed, and how keys and possession will be handled.
