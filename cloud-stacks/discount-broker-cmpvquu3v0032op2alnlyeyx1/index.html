But what does it actually entail, and how does it stack up against the traditional model? ### What Exactly is a "Discount Broker"? A discount brokerage is a business that charges clients significantly lower fees than a traditional, full-service brokerage (Wikipedia). They operate on a high-volume, low-margin business model, meaning they need to process many transactions to be profitable. This often translates to less time and fewer resources dedicated to each individual client. There are generally two flavors of discount brokers you'll encounter: * **Flat-Fee Brokers**: You pay a fixed price upfront for a specific menu of services. This almost always includes a basic listing on the local Multiple Listing Service (FMLS/GAMLS in Atlanta), but little else. * **Low-Commission Agents**: These agents work for a reduced commission percentage, perhaps 1% or 2% for the listing side. While they may offer more services than a flat-fee model, they often have to cut corners to make the numbers work. This contrasts sharply with a full-service Realtor, who operates on a standard commission basis but provides a comprehensive, end-to-end service designed to manage every aspect of your home sale. ### The Appeal: Why Atlanta Home Sellers Are Curious Let's be honest: the primary appeal is saving money. When you consider the robust Atlanta home prices, a standard 5-6% commission can feel like a substantial chunk of your hard-earned equity. For a homeowner in Buckhead, Midtown, or Decatur selling a $700,000 property, saving even 1-2% on commission translates to thousands of dollars. It's a completely logical first thought for any financially savvy homeowner. The question isn't whether saving on commission is appealing; it's whether those "savings" are real or just an illusion that costs you more in the end. ### What's Included vs. What's Left Out The core difference lies in the scope of services.
