How the 6% Commission is Typically Divided in a Georgia Real Estate Transaction

The traditional commission is not a single fee paid to one person. It's a pool of money, paid by you, the seller, that is divided between the brokerages representing both sides of the transaction. This structure was designed to incentivize cooperation among agents, ensuring a wide audience for your property.

Listing Agent's Share (Typically 3%): This fee goes to the brokerage of the agent you hire to represent you. It covers the cost of marketing your property, managing showings, providing expert advice, handling negotiations, and navigating the complex paperwork from listing to closing. This is the portion of the commission where savvy sellers can find immense savings.

Buyer's Agent's Share (Typically 3%): This fee is offered out of your proceeds to the agent who brings a qualified buyer to purchase your home. It serves as a powerful incentive for all local agents, whether they're in Atlanta proper or the surrounding suburbs, to show your property to their clients. Offering a competitive buyer's agent commission is a critical strategy for attracting the largest possible pool of potential buyers.

Beyond Commission: Other Common Seller Closing Costs in Atlanta

While commission is the largest expense, it's not the only one. When you sell your home in Georgia, your closing statement will include several other costs that are important to budget for. Understanding these helps you calculate your true net proceeds.

  • Georgia Transfer Tax: This is a state-mandated tax on the sale of property, calculated based on the home's sale price. It's a standard part of nearly every transaction in the state.
  • Title Insurance & Attorney Fees: Georgia is an "attorney state," which means a licensed real estate attorney must oversee the closing process to ensure all legal requirements are met. The seller often pays for the owner's title insurance policy for the buyer.
  • Prorated Property Taxes & HOA Dues: You are responsible for your share of property taxes and any Homeowners' Association dues for the portion of the year you owned the home, calculated up to the closing date.
  • Termite Letter (WDO Report) & Other Negotiated Costs: It's common for buyers to request a Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) report. Any costs for inspections or repairs agreed upon in the purchase contract will also appear on your closing statement.
