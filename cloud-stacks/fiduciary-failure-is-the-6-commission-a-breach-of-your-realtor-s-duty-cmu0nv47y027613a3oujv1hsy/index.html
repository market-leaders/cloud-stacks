The Core Conflict: Understanding Fiduciary Duty vs. The 6% Standard

A Realtor's fiduciary duty legally binds them to prioritize your financial interests above all else, a principle the rigid 6% commission model directly challenges. This foundational promise your agent makes is the bedrock of your relationship, yet the standard compensation structure introduces an immediate and unavoidable conflict.

What "Fiduciary Duty" Actually Means for Your Home Sale

When you hire a Realtor, they become your fiduciary. This isn't just a fancy term; it's a legal obligation composed of several key duties. While they all matter, one stands above the rest in this context. Loyalty: This is the cornerstone. Your agent must act solely in your best interest, which explicitly includes your financial well-being. Every piece of advice and every action taken must be to secure the best possible outcome for you, not to maximize their own paycheck. The client-first approach is not a suggestion; it's a requirement.

The Built-In Conflict of Interest with Percentage-Based Fees

A high, percentage-based commission creates a system where the agent's financial interests may not align with yours. Consider a simple scenario: your home is listed, and an offer comes in. You're considering a $10,000 price reduction to close the deal. For you, that $10,000 is a significant amount of equity. For an agent earning a 3% commission, their portion of that reduction is only $300. An agent might be less motivated to negotiate hard for that last $10,000 or advise you to hold out for a better offer when the personal financial incentive is so small. This structure can subtly encourage a quick sale over the highest possible price, which is a direct contradiction of their duty of loyalty.
