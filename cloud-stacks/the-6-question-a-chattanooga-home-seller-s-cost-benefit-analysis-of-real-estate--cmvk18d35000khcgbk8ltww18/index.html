Deconstructing the 6% Standard in the Chattanooga Market

Traditional real estate commissions in Chattanooga typically cost sellers 5-6% of the home's final sale price, significantly impacting their net proceeds.

This percentage might not sound like much at first, but when you apply it to local home values, the dollar amount can be staggering. It's often the single largest closing cost a seller will face.

The Real-Dollar Impact on Your Chattanooga Home Sale

Let's use a real-world example. According to Realtor.com, the median listing price for a home in Chattanooga hovers around $400,000. Applying the traditional commission structure to this price reveals the true cost.
  • Sale Price: $400,000
  • 6% Commission: $24,000
  • 5% Commission: $20,000
That $24,000 could be a down payment on an investment property, a new car, or a significant boost to your retirement savings. It’s a substantial amount of your equity handed over at the closing table.

How is the 6% Commission Actually Split?

A common misconception is that your agent keeps the full 6%. In reality, the seller pays the entire commission, which is then split between the two brokerages involved in the transaction.
  • Listing Brokerage (3%): This portion goes to the agent and brokerage representing you, the seller.
  • Buyer's Brokerage (3%): This portion is offered to the agent and brokerage who bring a qualified buyer to the table.
While these percentages are technically negotiable, the 5-6% total has been a long-standing industry norm.

Why Has 6% Been the Norm for So Long?

This pricing structure is largely a holdover from the pre-internet era. Before Zillow and online MLS access, brokerages were the sole gatekeepers of property information, relying on expensive print advertising and physical "listing books." The recent NAR commission settlement highlights how these historical practices are now being challenged, paving the way for more transparent and seller-friendly models.
