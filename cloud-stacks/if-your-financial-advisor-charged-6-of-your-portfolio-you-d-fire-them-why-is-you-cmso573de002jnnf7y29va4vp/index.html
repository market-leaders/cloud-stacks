The 6% Commission Is a Relic That Costs Homeowners a Fortune

The traditional 6% real estate commission is a flat-rate fee that often fails to reflect the actual work involved or the home's value. For decades, this percentage has been presented as the standard cost of selling a house, but when you scrutinize the numbers, the model begins to fall apart. It’s a system that benefits the industry far more than the consumer it’s supposed to serve.

The most direct way to understand the excessive nature of the 6% fee is to compare it to other professional financial services. Your home is an asset, just like your stock portfolio. But the fees for managing these assets are worlds apart.

Consider the fees on a $500,000 asset:

| Asset Type | Standard Fee | Calculation | Total Cost to You | | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | | **Home Sale** | 6% Commission | $500,000 x 6% | **$30,000** | | **Investment Portfolio** | 1% AUM Fee | $500,000 x 1% | **$5,000** |

While the investment fee is typically annual and the commission is a one-time transaction cost, the sheer magnitude of the difference is staggering. That $30,000 is stripped directly from your home equity—money that could have gone toward your next home, paid down debt, or bolstered your retirement savings. This fee structure has a significant impact on your net proceeds and can even affect your ability to avoid capital gains tax implications.

A Fee Structure Stuck in the Pre-Internet Era

Where did the 6% commission even come from? It became common in the mid-20th century, an era before the internet, Zillow, and digital marketing. Back then, agents incurred massive upfront costs. They spent fortunes on print advertising in newspapers, paid for expensive placements in thick MLS photo books, and spent countless hours physically driving to brokerages to share new listings.

Contrast that with today. The internet has completely disrupted real estate. A listing is syndicated to thousands of potential buyers across the nation in seconds via the MLS and sites like Realtor.com. Social media campaigns can target ideal buyers with pinpoint accuracy. Contracts are signed digitally, and virtual tours reduce the need for endless in-person showings. The agent's costs and time investment have plummeted, yet the 6% commission stubbornly remains.

Does Double the Price Mean Double the Work?

Here’s a question traditional agents don't want you to ask: Does an agent do twice the work to sell an $800,000 home versus a $400,000 home in the same neighborhood? The answer is almost always no. The core work—professional photography, creating an MLS listing, digital marketing, and managing contracts—is largely the same.

While a luxury agent might invest more in specialized marketing, the flat percentage model means their commission doubles from $24,000 to $48,000. The value delivered to the seller does not increase proportionally. This model disproportionately rewards agents for selling in high-priced markets, not for providing superior service. It's a system that has defied modern economic principles for far too long.
