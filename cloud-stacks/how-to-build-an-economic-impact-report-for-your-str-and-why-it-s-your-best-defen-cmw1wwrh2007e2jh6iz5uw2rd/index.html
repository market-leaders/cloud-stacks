How to Build an Economic Impact Report for Your STR (and Why It's Your Best Defense Against Bad Regulations)

An economic impact report proves your short-term rental's value to the community with hard data, giving you a powerful and credible voice against unfair regulations. It quantifies your contribution through guest spending, operational costs, and taxes. Your short-term rental is more than just a property; it's a small business that fuels your local economy. But when city councils debate new restrictions, that positive impact is often ignored, and hosts are painted as a problem. The single most powerful tool to change this narrative is an Economic Impact Report—a document that proves your value with undeniable data. As the owner association for short-term rental hosts, ASTRO provides the tools and network to not only create this report but to make your voice heard. This guide will show you how to build a compelling report that can protect your business and advocate for fair regulations.

Key Takeaways

  • An economic impact report transforms your STR from a perceived "nuisance" into a documented "local economic engine" in the eyes of policymakers.
  • The most effective reports quantify three key areas: direct guest spending at local businesses, your operational spending on local services, and the specific taxes you contribute.
  • Data alone isn't enough; amplifying your report's message through an organized owner network is critical for influencing local policy and seeing rule changes coming.

TL;DR

Your STR brings significant money into your community. An economic impact report proves it with hard data, giving you a powerful and credible voice against unfair regulations and helping you build alliances with other local businesses.

Why is an economic impact report my best defense against STR regulations?

An economic impact report transforms your personal story into hard data that city councils, neighborhood associations, and regulators cannot easily ignore. It reframes the conversation from anecdotes about noise or parking to a factual discussion about the jobs you support and the revenue you generate for your community. This data-driven approach provides a professional and credible foundation for all your advocacy efforts.

Shift the Narrative from Nuisance to Asset

Without data, regulators often focus on complaints. An economic impact report counters this by showcasing your property as a vital community asset. It demonstrates that your guests are spending money at local restaurants, shops, and attractions, directly supporting your neighbors and their livelihoods.

Speak the Language of Policymakers

City officials and staff respond to numbers, budgets, and tax revenue. Your report speaks their language. When you can present a clear, concise document showing exactly how much tax revenue you generate and how many local jobs you support, you move from being a line item on an agenda to a valued economic partner.

Build Alliances with Other Local Businesses

Your report is proof that your success is tied to the success of other local businesses. The coffee shop, the boutique, the tour guide—your guests are their customers. Sharing your data with these businesses helps them see you as an ally, not a competitor, creating a stronger, unified voice for fair tourism policies.

What key data should I include in my STR's economic impact report?

Your report should include direct guest spending at local businesses, your own operational spending with local vendors, and the specific taxes you generate for the community. By focusing on these three pillars of economic contribution, you create a comprehensive picture of the value your property brings to your town or city. This is the data that proves you are a vital part of the local economic fabric.

Step 1: Calculate Direct Guest Spending

This figure shows how your guests directly fuel the local economy. A simple post-stay survey can ask guests to estimate their spending on categories like dining, shopping, activities, and transportation. For a baseline, you can reference data from local or state tourism boards on average per-traveler spending. Highlight the specific local businesses you recommend in your welcome book or owner programs to make the connection tangible.

Step 2: Track Your Local Operational Spending

Compile a year's worth of invoices and receipts from all the local services you use to run your property. This includes your cleaning crew, landscaper, plumber, handyman, and pest control service. Don't forget purchases from local hardware stores, furniture shops, and supply companies. This data demonstrates how your business directly creates and sustains local jobs, turning abstract economic theory into paychecks for people in your community.

Step 3: Quantify Taxes and Fees Paid

This is the most direct financial contribution you make to your municipality. Document every dollar of transient occupancy tax (TOT), lodging tax, and sales tax you've collected and remitted. You should also calculate the portion of your property taxes that directly funds local schools, infrastructure projects, and public services. This number proves you are paying your fair share and contributing to the public good.

Step 4: Assemble Your Data into a Professional One-Page Report

Your final report should be clean, simple, and easy to understand in under a minute. Use clear headings, simple charts or graphs, and bullet points to highlight key numbers. Include a photo of your property and consider adding a testimonial from a local business owner you work with. Summarize everything into one powerful, headline number: the "Total Annual Economic Impact" of your property.

How can I use my report to effectively advocate for fair STR rules?

You should use your report to proactively engage with local officials and business groups *before* restrictive regulations are even proposed, establishing yourself as a community partner. When you combine your property's individual data with that of other owners, you create a powerful, collective voice that represents a significant economic bloc. This collective action, powered by the owner-first advocacy from ASTRO, turns individual reports into an undeniable advocacy movement.

Present Your Findings to Local Officials and Staff

Don't wait for a hearing to show up. Schedule brief, professional meetings with your city council members, the mayor's office, and planning department staff to introduce yourself and share your report. Frame yourself as a data-driven resource for them. When hearings do come up, your report can be submitted as official public comment, providing a factual anchor in an often emotional debate.

Share Your Report with Local Business Associations

Present your data to your local Chamber of Commerce, Downtown Business Association, or Restaurant Association. Show them the numbers that prove your guests are their customers. For example, if you own a rental in Breckenridge, showing the local ski shop how much your guests spend on rentals and gear makes you an indispensable partner. These organizations can become your most powerful allies in advocating for sensible regulations.
