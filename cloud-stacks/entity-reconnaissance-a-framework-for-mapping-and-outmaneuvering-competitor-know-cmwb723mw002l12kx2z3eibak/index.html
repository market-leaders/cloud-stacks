What is a Competitor's Knowledge Graph in Local SEO?

A competitor's knowledge graph is the network of interconnected entities, topics, and locations that Google understands about their business. It's the digital representation of their authority, expertise, and relevance within a specific market, built from data on their website and across the web.

Beyond Keywords: Thinking in Entities and Connections

For years, SEO focused on keyword strings. Today, search engines think in terms of entities and the relationships between them. A search query like "plumber near me" isn't just a string of words; it's a request involving the entities `Plumber` (a service), `[User's Location]` (a geo-entity), and the concept of `nearness` (a relationship). Google's objective is to understand these connections to provide the most authoritative answer, especially in AI Overviews.

Why This Matters More Than Rankings in the AI Era

A strong knowledge graph directly feeds Google's Knowledge Panels, Local Map Packs, and the new generative search experience. A well-defined entity strategy is crucial because AI is reshaping digital marketing by synthesizing information rather than just listing links. You're no longer just ranking a page; you're competing to become the canonical source of information for a topic in a specific geographic area.

Real-World Example: A Real Estate Brokerage's Knowledge Graph

A real estate brokerage isn't just one entity. It's a central node connected to a web of other entities that collectively prove its expertise and market authority. Consider these relationships:
  • Agent Entities: `John Doe` (an entity of type `RealEstateAgent`) `worksFor` `[Brokerage Name]`.
  • Neighborhood Entities: `[Brokerage Name]` (a `RealEstateBroker`) `servesArea` `[Downtown Neighborhood]`.
  • Property Type Entities: `John Doe` `specializesIn` `[Luxury Condos]`.
This network of verified connections proves expertise far more effectively than a page stuffed with keywords, forming the basis of a powerful SEO knowledge graph.

How Can I Map a Competitor's Entity Strategy?

You can map a competitor's entity strategy by systematically analyzing their structured data, content clusters, and real-world connections like local citations. This process of digital reconnaissance reveals the blueprint they are presenting to search engines.

Step 1: Deconstruct Their Digital Blueprint with Schema Analysis

Action: Use tools like Google's Rich Results Test or the Schema Markup Validator on your competitor's key pages, including their homepage, service pages, and location pages.

What to Look For: Are they using basic `LocalBusiness` schema, or are they defining more specific entities like `RealEstateAgent`, `MedicalClinic`, or `Service`? Look for advanced properties like `knowsAbout`, `servesArea`, or `department` that explicitly build connections between entities. The level of detail in their schema is a direct indicator of their entity SEO sophistication.

Step 2: Analyze Their Content Topography for Topic & Geo-Clusters

Action: Manually review their website or use a crawling tool to map their site structure and internal linking patterns.

What to Look For: First, identify their core service topics (e.g., "roof repair," "buyer representation"). More importantly, analyze how they use geo-modifiers. Do they have dedicated, in-depth pages for every city, neighborhood, or county they serve? The presence of these geo-clustered pages reveals the intended geographic scope of their knowledge graph.

Step 3: Trace Their Real-World Connections via Citations and Co-occurrence

Action: Analyze their Google Business Profile, local directory listings on sites like Yelp and industry-specific portals, and any press mentions.

What to Look For: Note the consistency of their Name, Address, and Phone Number (NAP), as this is a foundational signal. Look deeper for co-occurrence—what other local entities are they frequently mentioned alongside? For example, a home builder mentioned with a specific mortgage lender or a law firm mentioned with a local courthouse builds real-world entity connections that Google can understand. These are powerful offline signals that boost SEO.

How Do I Build a Stronger Knowledge Graph Than My Competitors?

You can build a stronger knowledge graph by creating topically deep, geographically specific content and reinforcing it with precise schema and consistent local signals. This involves identifying your competitors' weaknesses and building a more comprehensive and authoritative digital infrastructure.

Find the Gaps: Identify Their "Entity Voids"

Your reconnaissance work will reveal where your competitors are weak. These "entity voids" are your primary opportunities to establish dominance.
  • Example: Your competitor real estate agent has great content about the city as a whole but no dedicated pages for specific, high-value school districts or individual condo buildings. This is your opening to become the authority on those micro-entities.
  • Example: Your competitor contractor talks broadly about "kitchen remodeling" but has no content about specific sub-topics like "quartz countertop installation" or "custom cabinet design." These sub-topics are entity voids you can fill.

Build Your Geo-Entity Stack

This is where you go on the offensive. Instead of a single "Services" page, you must create a hierarchical content structure that maps directly to how users search and how Google understands the world. The strategic approach Dean Cacioppo implements for clients focuses on this granular build-out.

Structure: `[Service]` -> `[Service]` -> `[Service] in [Neighborhood]`.

Dean Cacioppo's Real Estate Insight: For brokerages, this means moving far beyond basic IDX listings. Create authoritative content for every neighborhood, school district, and condo development you serve, treating each as a distinct entity. Answer specific questions like, "What are the HOA fees at [Condo Building]?" or "What is the walk score of the Garden District?" This turns your website into an indispensable local resource.

Reinforce with Precision Schema and Internal Linking

For every piece of granular content you create, add specific schema markup to explicitly define its purpose for search engines. Use `FAQPage` schema for your neighborhood Q&As. Use `about` and `mentions` properties in your `LocalBusiness` schema to link to the neighborhood or service entities you're targeting. Then, use descriptive internal link anchor text to connect your entity pages, showing Google the relationships between them (e.g., link from your `Downtown` page to relevant agent profiles who specialize there). This technical infrastructure is key to making your AI-enhanced marketing effective.
