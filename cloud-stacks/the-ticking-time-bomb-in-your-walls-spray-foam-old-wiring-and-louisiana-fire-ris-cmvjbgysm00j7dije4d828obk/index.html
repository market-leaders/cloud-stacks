The Problem - Understanding the Hidden Fire Risk

Improperly installed spray foam insulation can create a serious fire hazard by trapping heat from outdated electrical wiring, a common issue in older Louisiana homes.

Why Old Wiring is a "Hot" Topic

Older electrical systems, particularly knob-and-tube or early ungrounded Romex wiring, were engineered with a specific feature in mind: open air. These systems were designed to dissipate the heat they generate into the empty cavities within your walls and attic. The insulation wrapping these wires was never intended to handle constant, high temperatures and can become brittle, crack, and fail over time, exposing live wires.

How Spray Foam Can Turn Up the Heat

Modern closed-cell spray foam is an exceptional insulator, which means it's excellent at stopping heat transfer. When sprayed directly over old wiring, it acts like a thick, thermal blanket. This prevents the wires from cooling down as designed, causing them to overheat far beyond their safety rating and dramatically increasing the risk of the wire's own insulation failing and igniting the surrounding wood framing.

Warning Signs Your Walls Are Overheating

Sometimes, a home will give subtle warnings of an electrical problem. You might notice frequently tripping breakers, lights that flicker for no reason, a faint burning smell near outlets, or discolored switch plates. More often than not, however, there are no warnings at all before a catastrophic fire starts within your walls.

The Cause - New Orleans' Unique Insulation Challenges

The historic housing stock and humid climate of the New Orleans area demand specialized insulation knowledge to avoid creating dangerous conditions within walls and attics.

Knob-and-Tube Nightmares in NOLA Homes

From the historic mansions of the Garden District to the classic shotguns in Mid-City, knob-and-tube wiring is a prevalent issue. This system is easily identifiable by its ceramic knobs and tubes that guide wires through wall studs. The National Electrical Code (NEC) explicitly forbids covering this type of wiring with any form of insulation, as it poses an undeniable fire risk.

Open-Cell vs. Closed-Cell: Why the Foam Type Matters

There are two main types of spray foam, and the choice is critical in our climate. While closed-cell foam presents a greater risk for heat encapsulation around wiring, it also provides a superior moisture barrier, which is essential for combating Louisiana's humidity. This creates a dilemma that only an expert can navigate safely, balancing energy efficiency with fire prevention and moisture control.

Louisiana Building Codes and Professional Liability

Hiring a licensed, insured, and experienced contractor is the only way to ensure your insulation job is done to code. An improper installation that leads to a fire can result in devastating liability for both the homeowner and the contractor. A professional understands these codes and will not risk your safety for a quick job.
