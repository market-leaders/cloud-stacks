Part 1: The Ground Rules - What Every American Buyer MUST Know

Your U.S. homeowner's policy is completely invalid for property in Mexico; you must secure a separate policy from a Mexican insurance carrier.

This is the first and most important rule. There are no exceptions, no workarounds, and no gray areas. Your policy from back home is geographically bound and stops at the border.

Why Your Allstate or State Farm Policy Won't Cut It in Cabo

It boils down to legal and jurisdictional boundaries. U.S. insurance companies are not licensed to underwrite property or assume risk in Mexico. They are considered "non-admitted carriers" and have no legal standing to pay out a claim on a property located here. Trying to use your U.S. policy for your Cabo San Lucas real estate is like trying to use pesos at a grocery store in Ohio—it just doesn't work.

The "Named Peril" Difference: What Mexican Policies Actually Cover

Most insurance policies in the U.S. are "all-risk," meaning they cover everything unless a peril is specifically excluded. In Mexico, the opposite is often true. Policies are typically "named peril," which means they only cover the risks, or "perils," that are explicitly listed. It's crucial to ensure your policy includes coverage for hydro-meteorological events (hurricanes, storms, floods), earthquakes, and civil liability.

Understanding Hurricane Deductibles: It's Not What You're Used To

Here's a wake-up call for many American buyers. In the event of a hurricane, your deductible is not a flat $1,000 or $2,500. Instead, it's calculated as a percentage, typically between 2-5%, of the total insured value of your property. Let's do the math: on a $1M home with a 5% hurricane deductible, your out-of-pocket cost is $50,000 before the insurance company pays a single dime. This is a critical "No-BS" fact you need to budget for.

Key Coverage for Your Property in Cabo San Lucas, San Jose del Cabo, or the East Cape

Where your property is located heavily influences your insurance needs. In bustling areas like Cabo San Lucas and San Jose del Cabo, condo owners must understand the difference between their HOA's "master policy" and their individual "contents" policy. For those with East Cape real estate or homes in more remote communities like Zacatitos, robust hurricane coverage for exposed, single-family homes is non-negotiable, with add-ons for things like solar panels or custom pools.

The Role of Your Fideicomiso (Bank Trust) in Your Insurance Policy

When buying a home in Cabo San Lucas as a foreigner, you'll likely use a bank trust, or fideicomiso. It is absolutely essential that your insurance policy is structured to name both you (the beneficiary) and the bank holding the trust. This is a common and costly mistake that an experienced real estate team helps you avoid, ensuring your fideicomiso is properly protected.
