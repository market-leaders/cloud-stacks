Understanding the Fear: Deconstructing the Northshore Real Estate Commission

The fear of a "boycott" stems from a misunderstanding of how real estate commissions are structured on the Northshore.

The anxiety around selling with a low cost real estate broker is almost always rooted in confusion about who gets paid what. The total commission is not one lump sum; it's split into two distinct parts. Understanding this split is the key to dispelling the boycott myth.

The Two Sides of the Commission Coin: Listing Fee vs. Buyer Agent Commission (BAC)

When you see a "6% commission," it's not going entirely to one agent. It's divided between the brokerage representing the seller and the brokerage representing the buyer. Here’s how it works:

  • Listing Broker Fee: This is the portion you pay your agent for marketing and selling your home. This is our 1%. It covers professional photography, MLS entry, signage, marketing, negotiations, and expert guidance from contract to close.
  • Buyer Agent Commission (BAC): This is the portion you offer to the agent who brings the successful buyer. This is a crucial marketing tool advertised on the Multiple Listing Service (MLS) for all agents to see.

You Control What the Buyer's Agent Earns

Here is the most important fact: the seller—with our expert guidance—chooses the BAC. Our 1% fee does not impact the buyer's agent's potential earnings whatsoever. We advise our Northshore clients to offer a competitive BAC, typically between 2.5% and 3%, to incentivize every agent from Slidell to Hammond to bring their qualified buyers.

A buyer's agent looking at the MLS sees the compensation you're offering them directly. They don't see our 1% fee, nor would it matter if they did. Their focus is on the commission offered for bringing a buyer and, most importantly, on finding the right home for their client.

Fiduciary Duty: The Agent's #1 Responsibility is to Their Buyer

This is the cornerstone of the agent-client relationship. A buyer's agent has a fiduciary duty to act in their client's best financial interest. Intentionally hiding a perfect home from their client because they don't like the listing agent's business model is a serious violation of this duty. It exposes that agent to potential lawsuits from their own client and disciplinary action from the Louisiana Real Estate Commission, including the loss of their license.
