Many homebuyers are surprised to learn that a simple flood map designation isn't the end of the story. The actual elevation of your home is what truly determines your risk and costs, which is where the critical documents of your real estate transaction come into play. ### The Role of the Land Survey and Elevation Certificate It's easy to confuse the different types of surveys, but for flood purposes, one is particularly important. * **Boundary Survey**: This survey defines your property lines, showing the legal boundaries of the land you are purchasing. It identifies any potential encroachments or issues with neighboring properties. * **Elevation Certificate (EC)**: This document, prepared by a licensed surveyor, details a building's location, lowest floor elevation, and building characteristics relative to the Base Flood Elevation (BFE). The BFE is the height to which floodwater is anticipated to rise during a 1% annual chance flood event. Your lender requires an Elevation Certificate in high-risk zones to confirm the property's risk level and ensure their investment is protected by the correct amount of flood insurance. Your property's history matters. A flood zone can place specific legal requirements on a property, and our job is to ensure there are no hidden issues tied to the land you're about to own. During our comprehensive search, we specifically look for: * **Floodplain or Drainage Easements**: We identify any parts of your property that cannot be built upon due to water management requirements. An easement could significantly restrict your plans for a future pool or home addition. * **Previous LOMAs or LOMRs**: We check for a Letter of Map Amendment (LOMA) or Letter of Map Revision (LOMR). These are official documents from FEMA that may have changed the property's flood zone designation in the past based on new data or construction. * **Ensuring Clear Title**: We confirm there are no outstanding liens or common title issues related to past flood damage or rebuilding efforts, which could become your financial responsibility after closing. ### Decoding Flood-Related Fees on Your Closing Disclosure Buyers in St. Tammany Parish should anticipate specific flood-related closing costs, and understanding these upfront is key to a smooth experience. No one likes surprises at the closing table. These fees are standard and necessary to protect all parties involved. * **Flood Zone Determination Fee**: This is a mandatory fee paid to a specialized third-party vendor. They use official FEMA maps and data to certify the property's official flood zone for the lender, a step required for nearly every mortgage. * **Survey & Elevation Certificate Costs**: This is the fee paid to a licensed surveyor to perform the fieldwork and produce the required documents. Costs can vary based on the property's size and complexity. * **Prepaid Flood Insurance Premium**: Lenders require you to pay the first full year of your flood insurance policy premium at or before closing.
