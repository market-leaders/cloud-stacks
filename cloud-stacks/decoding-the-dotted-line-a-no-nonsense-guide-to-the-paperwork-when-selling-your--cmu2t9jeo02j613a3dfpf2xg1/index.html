The Starting Line - Pre-Listing Paperwork in Kentucky

The journey to selling your Louisville home officially begins with the Listing Agreement and key property disclosures. This initial paperwork establishes your professional relationship with your agent and fulfills Kentucky's legal requirements for transparency before your home even hits the market.

The Exclusive Right-to-Sell Listing Agreement

What it is: This is the contract that formally hires your real estate brokerage. It’s the handshake in writing, giving your agent the exclusive right to market and sell your property for a specific period. It outlines the duties of the agent and the seller, ensuring everyone is on the same page from day one.

Key Terms to Look For:

  • Listing Price: The agreed-upon asking price for your home. Getting this right is a blend of art and science, and a key part of how to price your home correctly.
  • Contract Duration: The length of the agreement, typically ranging from 90 to 180 days. This gives your agent adequate time to market the property effectively.
  • Commission: This is where you'll see our 1% listing fee clearly stated, alongside the commission you agree to offer the buyer's agent. The expert guidance from 1 Percent Lists Purple Door makes this process transparent from the very start.

The Kentucky Seller's Disclosure of Property Condition

Louisville-Specific Context
This is a state-mandated form in Kentucky, and it's not optional. Think of it as your home’s official autobiography, written by you. It’s one of the most important documents you'll handle before listing.
Purpose
This form protects both you and the buyer by providing a detailed, honest history of the property. You'll answer a comprehensive list of questions about the roof's age, the HVAC system's condition, plumbing issues, foundation integrity, and any other known defects, past or present. It’s your legal obligation to be truthful.
Pro-Tip
Honesty is always the best policy here. Attempting to hide a known issue, like a basement that occasionally gets damp after a heavy Louisville thunderstorm, can lead to serious legal and financial trouble after the sale closes. We'll guide you through filling this out accurately to protect your interests.

Lead-Based Paint Disclosure

Who it applies to
This is a federal requirement for any home built before 1978. Given the historic charm and architecture in many of Louisville's best neighborhoods like the Highlands, Old Louisville, and Germantown, this form is very common.
What it does
It's simpler than it sounds. You simply state whether you have any knowledge of lead-based paint or lead-based paint hazards in the home. You also must provide the buyer with any records you have and an official EPA pamphlet. This is a simple but critical compliance step.

The Main Event - Deciphering the Purchase Agreement

The Purchase Agreement is the most important document in your Louisville home sale, outlining every term of the transaction between you and the buyer. Once an offer comes in, this legally binding contract becomes the roadmap for the entire sale, and understanding its key components is non-negotiable for a successful outcome.

Breaking Down the Louisville Area Purchase Contract

When you receive an offer, it will be on a standardized form, but the details within it are what matter. This is where a great agent proves their worth, helping you analyze not just the price, but the entire package.

Key Components:

  • Offer Price: The big number everyone looks at first. Is it what you hoped for? Is it competitive?
  • Earnest Money Deposit: This is the "good faith" deposit the buyer makes to show they are serious. It's typically held in escrow by the title company and applied to the buyer's down payment at closing.
  • Contingencies: These are the conditions that must be met for the sale to proceed. Common ones include the financing contingency (the buyer must secure a loan), the inspection contingency (the buyer must be satisfied with the home inspection), and the appraisal contingency (the home must appraise for at least the purchase price). We'll help you analyze the strength of these contingencies to gauge the true quality of the offer.
  • Closing Date: The target date for the final transfer of ownership. This date is negotiable and needs to work for both parties' timelines.

Navigating Counteroffers and Addendums

The first offer is rarely the final one. The initial purchase agreement often serves as the opening line in a negotiation. We'll help you draft a counteroffer to negotiate the price, who pays for closing costs, the closing date, or other terms to better suit your needs.

Any changes or additions to the original contract are handled through official addendums, which are signed by both parties. This ensures every detail of the agreement is in writing, leaving no room for "he said, she said" confusion down the road. This meticulous management is a core part of the "full service" we provide.

The Home Inspection Response

After the home inspection, a buyer may present you with a list of requested repairs. This is a common hurdle and essentially a second round of negotiations. Some requests are reasonable; others might be over the top.

Our role as your agent is to help you assess the buyer's requests, get estimates for potential repairs, and negotiate a solution that protects your interests without derailing the entire sale. Whether it’s agreeing to fix a few key items or offering a credit at closing, we'll find a path forward.

The ALTA Statement / Settlement Statement

What it is
This is the final balance sheet of the transaction, meticulously prepared by the title company or closing attorney. It itemizes every single credit (money to you) and debit (money you owe) for both the buyer and the seller. It's the official scorecard of the entire deal.
What to Look For
You'll see the final sales price on the credit side. On the debit side, you'll see your remaining mortgage payoff, prorated property taxes, any seller concessions or repair credits, and all closing costs—including the real estate commissions. We review this document with you line by line to ensure its accuracy before you sign.

The Deed and Final Signatures

The Deed
This is the legal instrument that officially transfers the title of the property from you (the grantor) to the new owner (the grantee). You will sign this at the closing table, and it will be officially recorded with the Jefferson County Clerk's office.
The Closing Table
You'll sign the deed, the settlement statement, and a few other affidavits. Once the buyer's funds have been received and all documents are signed, the transaction is complete. You hand over the keys, and you've officially sold your home!
