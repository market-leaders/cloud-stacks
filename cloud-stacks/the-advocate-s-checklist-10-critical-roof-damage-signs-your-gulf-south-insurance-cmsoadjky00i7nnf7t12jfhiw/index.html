The Advocate's Checklist: 10 Overlooked Damage Points

Here are 10 critical signs of roof damage that a trained local expert will find, even if your initial insurance report doesn't.

1. Wind-Lifted or "Creased" Shingles

What it is: Shingles that were lifted by wind but didn't fly off. They have a subtle horizontal crease where the sealant strip was broken, compromising their water-shedding ability. This is one of the most common signs of wind damage.

Why Adjusters Miss It: They're looking for missing shingles, not compromised ones. It requires manually lifting shingles to check the seal, which takes time they often don't have.

Why It Matters: A broken seal is a guaranteed future leak. A roof with widespread broken seals has lost its integrity and often qualifies for a full replacement.

2. Subtle Hail "Bruising" on Asphalt

What it is: Hail doesn't always puncture a shingle. Often, it creates a soft "bruise" by displacing the protective granules and weakening the underlying asphalt mat. It feels like a soft spot on an apple.

Why Adjusters Miss It: It's not visible from the ground and requires a hands-on, tactile inspection. They may mark it as "cosmetic" when it's actually functional damage.

Why It Matters: These bruises accelerate the shingle's aging, making it brittle and prone to cracking and leaking within a year or two.

3. Granule Loss in Gutters and Downspouts

What it is: A significant collection of the sand-like granules from your shingles found in your gutters.

Why Adjusters Miss It: They often don't check the gutters. They might dismiss it as normal "aging" if they do see it.

Why It Matters: Heavy granule loss after a storm is the #1 sign of widespread hail impact or intense wind "scrubbing." Those granules are your roof's primary defense against UV rays; without them, the roof will fail rapidly.

4. Dented or Damaged Soft Metals (Vents, Flashing, Drip Edge)

What it is: Dents and dings on roof vents, pipe boots, and the metal flashing in roof valleys or around chimneys.

Why Adjusters Miss It: They may focus only on the main field of shingles and ignore these "accessories."

Why It Matters: This is undeniable proof of hail size and storm intensity. If the metal is dented, the shingles were hit with the same force, which is key evidence to argue against a "cosmetic damage" ruling.

5. Popped Nails

What it is: Nails that have been slightly pushed up from the roof deck due to wind pressure lifting the shingle.

Why Adjusters Miss It: It's a tiny detail that's easy to overlook during a quick walk-through.

Why It Matters: A popped nail is a direct pathway for water to enter your attic. It's also a sign that the shingle is no longer properly secured and is vulnerable to being torn off in the next storm.

6. Cracked Sealant Around Penetrations

What it is: The caulk or sealant around pipe boots, vents, and flashing that has cracked or pulled away.

Why Adjusters Miss It: They consider it a "maintenance issue," not storm damage.

Why It Matters: High winds cause the entire roof system to flex and shift, which is what breaks these seals. It's direct evidence of the storm's force and a major source of hard-to-find leaks.

7. Interior Water Stains on Ceilings or in the Attic

What it is: Faint, yellowish, or brownish stains on your ceiling, or damp insulation/wood in your attic.

Why Adjusters Miss It: Most adjusters never look inside the house or attic unless you specifically point out a major, active leak.

Why It Matters: This is the ultimate proof that the roofing system has failed. It connects the exterior damage directly to interior consequences, strengthening your claim significantly.

8. Collateral Damage (Damaged Siding, Window Screens, Gutters)

What it is: Damage to other parts of your property, like torn window screens, dented siding, or cracked gutters.

Why Adjusters Miss It: Their scope is often limited to "the roof" only.

Why It Matters: This helps paint a complete picture of the storm's severity. If the wind was strong enough to shred a screen, it was certainly strong enough to break shingle seals.

9. "Zippering" or Unlocked Shingles

What it is: On architectural or dimensional shingles, this is when the overlapping layers become unseated from each other due to wind uplift.

Why Adjusters Miss It: The roof can look fine from a distance. It requires a close-up inspection to see that the shingle courses are no longer locked together.

Why It Matters: This is a critical structural failure of the shingle system, leaving large sections of your roof vulnerable to being peeled back by the next gust of wind.

10. Micro-Fractures on the Underside of Decking

What it is: Tiny stress cracks in the plywood or OSB roof decking, visible only from inside the attic.

Why Adjusters Miss It: They almost never inspect the decking from the attic.

Why It Matters: This is expert-level evidence. It shows that hail impacts were so severe they transferred energy through the shingle and underlayment to fracture the wood, proving the damage is far more than cosmetic.
