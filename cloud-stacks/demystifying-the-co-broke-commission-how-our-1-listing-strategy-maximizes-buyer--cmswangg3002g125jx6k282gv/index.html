The Foundation: Understanding Traditional Commission Structures

Most Castle Rock homeowners traditionally pay a 5-6% total commission, which is split between their agent and the buyer's agent. This long-standing model has become a significant financial burden for sellers, especially as home values in Douglas County have appreciated. Understanding this structure is the first step to seeing how much you can save.

What is a Co-Broke Commission? The "Buyer's Agent" Fee Explained

Co-Broke Commission: This is the portion of the total commission the seller agrees to pay the real estate agent who brings the successful buyer to the transaction. Its primary purpose is to incentivize the thousands of agents across the Denver Metro area to actively find buyers for and show your Castle Rock property. It's a powerful marketing tool paid at closing.

The Standard Commission Split in the Douglas County Market

In a typical transaction, a 6% total commission is often split right down the middle: 3% goes to the listing brokerage and 3% goes to the buyer's brokerage. On a $750,000 home, a common price point in many Castle Rock neighborhoods, a 6% commission totals a staggering $45,000. Of that, $22,500 goes to the listing agent and $22,500 goes to the buyer's agent. This is the number we are about to slash.
