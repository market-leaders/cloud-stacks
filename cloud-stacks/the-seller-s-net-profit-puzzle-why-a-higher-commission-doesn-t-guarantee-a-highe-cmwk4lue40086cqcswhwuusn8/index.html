The belief that a higher fee incentivizes an agent to work harder is a relic of a bygone era, before technology leveled the playing field. ### The Traditional Commission Assumption For years, the prevailing logic was that a higher commission percentage motivated agents to secure a better deal for their clients. The thinking goes, "If I pay more, they'll try harder." However, the financial reality for an individual agent doesn't support this. The difference in an agent's take-home pay between a sale price of $390,000 and $400,000 is minimal, often just a few hundred dollars after brokerage splits. A dedicated professional's primary driver isn't that small margin; it's their professional reputation, their fiduciary duty to you, and the desire to earn future referrals by achieving excellent results. ### Where Does the Commission Actually Go? A total commission is typically split multiple ways. It's first divided between the brokerage representing the seller (the listing brokerage) and the brokerage representing the buyer. Then, each of those brokerages pays their respective agents a portion of that fee. When you list your home, you decide on the listing fee for your agent and the amount you will offer to the agent who brings a qualified buyer. The crucial takeaway is that the powerful marketing tools that get homes sold are not exclusive to agents who charge more.
