While keywords are simple strings of text, entities are the building blocks of understanding for a machine. **Entity**: A distinct, well-defined concept—a person, place, organization, or idea—that a machine can understand and categorize. According to Wikipedia, an entity is something that exists as itself, which is precisely how knowledge graphs treat brands and concepts. A keyword like "best plumber" is just a character string. An entity strategy connects the brand `[Your Plumbing Co.]` to the service entity `[Emergency Plumbing]` and the location entity `[Brooklyn]`, with attributes like `[24/7 Availability]` and `[Certified Technicians]`. This web of connections forms a knowledge graph, and it's the raw material AI engines use to construct answers. ### If You Aren't Defining Your Entities, the AI Will Let Your Competitors Define Them for You In the absence of clear, authoritative information from you, LLMs will rely on whatever data they can find—which is often your competitor's well-structured content. This is how market leaders are dethroned in AI answers overnight. Your decade of #1 rankings means little if your content isn't formatted for machine consumption. This isn't a black box. It's a technical process of auditing, analyzing, and mapping. Follow these three steps for any competitor you see cited in an LLM answer. ### Step 1: The Audit - Prompt the AI Like a Customer The first step is to gather raw intelligence by querying AI engines just as your ideal customer would. Use a mix of tools like Perplexity, ChatGPT, and Google's AI Overviews to get a broad view of the information landscape. Ask 10-15 high-intent questions that cover the full purchase funnel. * **Informational:** "What is the difference between residential and commercial HVAC?" or "What are the signs of a failing roof?" * **Navigational:** "Who is the top-rated roofer in Austin, TX?" or "Find a 24/7 emergency plumber near me." * **Transactional:** "How much does it cost to repair a foundation leak?" or "Get a quote for weekly office cleaning services." Meticulously document every single source the AI cites for its answers. A simple spreadsheet will do. This list of URLs is your starting point—the exact pages that have successfully taught the AI to associate a brand with a concept. ### Step 2: The Teardown - Analyze the Cited Content Structure Next, open the competitor pages that were cited. The key is to ignore the prose and marketing fluff. Instead, view the page as a machine would, looking for explicit structural signals that make information easy to parse and extract. * **Definition Lists (`
`) & Tables:** Are they explicitly defining key industry terms? Machines love this format because it creates a clear subject-object relationship (e.g., "Cloud Stacking: A method of..."). This is a direct way to feed a knowledge graph. * **FAQ & HowTo Schema:** Check the page source for structured data. Are they using schema to spoon-feed answers to Google and other engines? This is no longer a "nice-to-have"; it's a primary communication channel with AI. * **Concise H2s/H3s:** Read their headings. Do they make standalone, quotable claims that could be lifted directly into an AI answer? Vague headings like "Our Process" are useless. Headings like "Foundation Repair Costs Range from $2,000 to $7,000" are goldmines for AI extraction. * **Internal Linking:** Pay close attention to their anchor text. Are they linking entity keywords (e.g., "our commercial HVAC services") directly to the corresponding service page? This reinforces the relationship between the conceptual entity and the service entity within their own site structure. This teardown reveals *how* they are communicating with machines, not just humans. ### Step 3: The Blueprint - Map Their Entity Connections Finally, translate your findings into a visual map. Use a whiteboard, a Miro board, or a spreadsheet to blueprint the competitor's strategy. This makes their abstract strategy tangible and actionable. * **Core Entity:** `[Competitor Brand Name]` * **Connected Service Entities:** `[Service A]`, `[Service B]`, `[Service C]` (e.g., `[Roof Repair]`, `[Gutter Installation]`) * **Connected Location Entities:** `your city`, `[Neighborhood]`, `[Service Area]` (e.g., `[Austin]`, `[Travis County]`) * **Connected Conceptual Entities:** `[Affordability]`, `[24/7 Support]`, `[Eco-Friendly]`, `[Licensed & Insured]` This map is their entire game plan, laid bare. It shows you precisely which concepts they have successfully associated with their brand in the web's collective machine consciousness. Your job is to now build a stronger, clearer, and more interconnected map for your own brand. ## The Scalable Solution: How to Automate and Distribute Your New Entity Strategy Knowing your competitor's strategy is useless if you can't execute your own at scale.
