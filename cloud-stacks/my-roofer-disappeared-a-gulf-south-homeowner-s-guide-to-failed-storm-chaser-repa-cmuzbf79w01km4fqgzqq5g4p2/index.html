A Failed Storm Chaser Repair Often Leaves Behind Telltale Signs of Shoddy Workmanship.

Before you can find a solution, you need to understand the scope of the problem. Out-of-town contractors often cut corners in predictable ways, and identifying these red flags can help you explain the issue to a reputable roofer and your insurance company.

Common Red Flags of a Botched Roofing Job

  • Mismatched or "Nailed Over" Shingles: Look for shingles that don't match in color or style, or new shingles simply nailed over the old, damaged ones. This is a lazy shortcut that traps moisture and accelerates rot.
  • Reused or Improperly Sealed Flashing: Flashing—the metal strips around chimneys, vents, and skylights—is a common failure point. Chasers often reuse old, bent flashing or simply smear roofing cement over it, which is a temporary fix doomed to fail in the Gulf South's heat and rain. Proper roof flashing installation is critical.
  • New Leaks or Persistent Drips: The most obvious sign. If the original leak is back or new ones have appeared, the repair failed to address the root cause. You need an expert who knows how to find the true source of a roof leak.
  • Improper Nailing: Look for exposed nail heads, "high nails" that don't sit flat, or nails driven straight through the shingle. All of these create new entry points for water and are clear signs of poor workmanship.

Your Immediate Action Plan: What to Do Right Now

  1. Document Everything: Take clear, well-lit photos and videos of the leaking areas inside and the shoddy work on the roof outside. Save all receipts, contracts, and records of communication with the original roofer.
  2. Mitigate Further Damage: Place buckets to catch drips to protect your floors and belongings. If the leak is severe, you may need professional emergency roof repairs or tarping to prevent catastrophic water damage.
  3. Contact a Reputable Local Inspector: Do not attempt to fix it yourself. You need a professional assessment from a company that understands Gulf South building codes and weather challenges.
