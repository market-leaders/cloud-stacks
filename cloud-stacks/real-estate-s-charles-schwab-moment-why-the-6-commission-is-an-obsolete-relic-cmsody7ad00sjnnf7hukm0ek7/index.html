The 6% Commission: An Outdated Tradition Costing You Thousands

The standard 6% real estate commission is a relic from a pre-internet era when finding buyers and marketing a home required immense manual effort and exclusive access to information. This model, which has stubbornly resisted change for decades, is now facing a long-overdue reckoning. It’s a system where the fees are simply too high for the value provided in today's technologically advanced world.

Where Did the 6% Commission Come From?

The 6% commission model solidified in the 1940s and 50s, an era vastly different from our own. Back then, a real estate agent's primary role was that of an information gatekeeper. They held the keys to the kingdom: the physical MLS book, a hefty tome that was the only source for property listings. There was no Zillow, no Realtor.com, no instant access to market data. Marketing was an expensive, manual, and time-consuming process. It involved placing costly newspaper ads, printing and distributing flyers, and spending hours on the phone. This high-touch, low-tech environment created a justification for a higher fee structure. The commission wasn't just for the agent's expertise; it was to cover the significant overhead and inefficiency of a pre-digital industry.
