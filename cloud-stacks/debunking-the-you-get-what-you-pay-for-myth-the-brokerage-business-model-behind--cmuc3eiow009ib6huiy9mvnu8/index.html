The Truth About the 6% Commission in the Atlanta Real Estate Market

Traditional real estate commissions in Atlanta are not fixed by law and typically cost sellers 5-6% of their home's sale price, significantly impacting their net proceeds.

For decades, the 5-6% commission has been the default setting in real estate transactions across the country, and the Atlanta market is no exception. This figure became so common that many homeowners mistakenly believe it's a mandatory, non-negotiable fee. However, this is simply not true. Commissions have always been negotiable, and recent industry shifts, like the NAR settlement, are bringing this fact into the spotlight, empowering sellers to question the status quo.

How a Typical 6% Commission is Split in Georgia

One of the most common points of confusion for sellers is understanding where all that money goes. A traditional 6% commission isn't paid to a single agent. Instead, it's typically split down the middle between two different brokerages.

  • ~3% to the Listing Brokerage: This is the brokerage that represents you, the seller. From this 3%, they pay their agent (the listing agent), cover marketing costs, and pay for their brokerage's overhead.
  • ~3% to the Buyer's Brokerage: This is the brokerage representing the person who buys your home. They, in turn, pay their agent (the buyer's agent) from this portion.

The critical takeaway here is that you, the seller, pay the entire commission from your proceeds at closing. This includes the fee for the agent whose primary duty is to represent the buyer's best interests, not yours. This structure has long been the standard, but it places a substantial financial burden squarely on the seller's shoulders.
